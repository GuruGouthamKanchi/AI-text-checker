\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{R}{ecent} advancements in deep learning have revolutionized natural language understanding and automated text generation \cite{vaswani2017attention}. However, the prolific adoption of LLMs in academic manuscript preparation presents profound challenges for scientific integrity and peer-review publishing \cite{solaiman2019release}. As demonstrated by Mitchell et al. \cite{mitchell2023detectgpt}, machine-generated text resides in localized negative curvature regions of the log-probability density function:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:curvature}
\mathbf{d}(x, P_\theta) = \log P_\theta(x) - \mathbb{E}_{\tilde{x} \sim q(\cdot|x)} \left[ \log P_\theta(\tilde{x}) \right]
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{x}$ represents minor local token perturbations obtained via mask-filling. 

Consequently, traditional humanizers attempt to lower detection probability by aggressively substituting synonyms. However, as proven by Sadasivan et al. \cite{sadasivan2024can}, unconstrained paraphrasing causes severe corruption of domain terminology, citation keys, and mathematical proofs. To overcome these limitations, it is imperative to establish a closed-loop dual-agent architecture with strict Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) node locking.

\section{Methodology}
Our framework consists of three unified modules: (i) LaTeX AST Parsing and Node Isolation, (ii) Dual-Agent Closed-Loop Optimization, and (iii) Semantics-Preserving Verification.

\subsection{LaTeX AST Node Protection}
The input LaTeX document $\mathcal{D}_{\text{tex}}$ is parsed into a sequence of structural nodes $\mathcal{N} = \{n_1, n_2, \dots, n_k\}$. We partition $\mathcal{N}$ into \textit{Immutable Lock Nodes} $\mathcal{N}_{\text{lock}}$ and \textit{Mutable Text Paragraph Nodes} $\mathcal{N}_{\text{text}}$:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:joint_loss}
\mathcal{N}_{\text{lock}} &= \mathcal{N}_{\text{math}} \cup \mathcal{N}_{\text{table}} \cup \mathcal{N}_{\text{media}} \cup \mathcal{N}_{\text{cite}} \\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{joint}}(y) &= \alpha \cdot \left(1 - P_{\text{AI}}(y)\right) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \text{Sim}_{\text{SciBERT}}(x, y)
\end{align}
where $x \in \mathcal{N}_{\text{text}}$ denotes the original paragraph, $y$ is a candidate paraphrase, and $\alpha = 0.55$ balances detection evasion against semantic fidelity.
