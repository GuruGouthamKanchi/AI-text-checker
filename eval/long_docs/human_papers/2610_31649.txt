Abstract
Single-image 3D generation has achieved high fidelity on curated benchmarks, yet degrades sharply on real-
world images with uneven illumination, blur, and occlusions. Purifying such inputs requires deciding when
to intervene and preserving the visual cues needed for 3D generation, while leaving clean inputs unchanged.
We introduce Gate3DGen, a selective pre-processing model that gates image inputs to existing 3D generators
without retraining them. A degradation-aware router bypasses inputs classified as clean and extracts features
to guide purification of the remaining inputs. Conditioned on these features, a 3D-oriented one-step purifier
purifies degraded inputs with spatial losses that encourage preservation of object identity, contours, and textural
cues. To provide paired supervision, we construct a synthetic data engine that renders clean views of 3D assets
and synthesises composite degradations. On the synthetic benchmark, the purifier improves image fidelity and
downstream 3D geometry in only 2.3 s on average, reducing TRELLIS.2 mesh distance by 61.5% relative to de-
graded inputs. VLM evaluation and human preferences on real photographs further support identity preservation
and degradation correction. More demos and code are available at supra-3d.github.io/gate3dgen.

1 Introduction
Single-image 3D generative models have achieved remarkable progress on curated benchmarks (Hong et al., 2024;
Tochilkin et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024; Xiang et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026), yet degrade sharply on
real-world images. This vulnerability stems from restrictive assumptions about input images. Contemporary models
assume centered, sharp, and fully visible objects under uniform studio lighting (Xu et al., 2024; Xiang et al., 2025;
2026). In real-world scenarios, however, unconstrained photographs routinely exhibit blur, uneven illumination, or
occlusions (Cho et al., 2025; Qu et al., 2026), corrupting visual cues and inducing severe artifacts in 3D outputs.
Open-source image-to-3D pipelines commonly use background removal and recentering (Tochilkin et al., 2024; Xu
et al., 2024; Xiang et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025), which do not directly address photometric corruptions or foreground
occlusions. Specialized preprocessing also targets individual factors such as illumination through delighting (Li et al.,
2025a; Zhao et al., 2025). When multiple degradations coexist, correcting one factor can leave other sources of ambiguity
in the conditioning image. Our comparisons show that generic 2D image editing can alter object appearance or contours,
with adverse effects on downstream geometry (Section 4.4). The objective is therefore to recover a suitable observation
of the same object, retaining the visible structural evidence that the 3D generator uses to infer shape and appearance.
This motivates selective input purification that preserves visual cues for 3D generation while avoiding unnecessary
changes to already suitable clean inputs.
We therefore introduce Gate3DGen, a selective pre-processing model that gates real-world images to existing 3D gener-
ators (Figure 1). It routes each image through purification or an unchanged bypass, keeping the downstream generators
frozen. Gate3DGen couples a degradation-aware router with a 3D-oriented one-step purifier. The router bypasses inputs
classified as clean and selects the remaining inputs for purification, while extracting continuous degradation-aware fea-
tures for the purifier. Conditioned on these features, the one-step purifier purifies degraded inputs in a single forward pass
to reduce inference cost, with spatial losses that encourage preservation of object identity and essential geometric and tex-
tural cues. Training requires matched ground-truth references, which are unavailable for real-world images. To provide
paired training supervision without manual collection, we construct a synthetic data engine from 3D assets. Rendering
multi-view anchors from 3,961 3D assets (Zhang et al., 2025) and synthesizing composite corruptions (e.g., uneven
illumination, blur, and occlusions) yields 43,936 aligned image pairs and 1,082 held-out cases with 3D ground truth.
We evaluate the purifier on this synthetic benchmark with two pretrained 3D generative models, TRELLIS.2 (Xiang
et al., 2026) and Pixal3D (Li et al., 2026). For 2D purification, Gate3DGen reduces foreground LPIPS by 39.1% while
preserving subject silhouettes, with an inference time of 2.3 s per image. In comparison, the image editing baselines
require 15.6–100.8 s per image and can alter object identity and reduce reconstruction fidelity (Table 2). When used to
condition these 3D generative models, the purified images consistently improve all geometry and appearance metrics
over the original observations. Specifically, Gate3DGen achieves a 61.5% reduction in all-surface mesh distance, closes
55.5% of the geometry gap to clean references on average (up to 83.1% on TRELLIS.2), and reduces rendering LPIPS
by 10.9%. Evaluated separately, the degradation-aware router identifies degraded inputs with 88.45% recall and 93.47%
specificity on clean inputs, sending inputs classified as degraded for purification. On real photographs, VLM scores and
human preferences further support identity preservation and degradation correction, complementing the reference-based
evaluation on synthetic inputs.
Our contributions are as follows:
• We formulate robust single-image 3D generation as selective input purification, improving existing 3D generators
through purified inputs without retraining them.
• We introduce Gate3DGen, combining a router that bypasses inputs classified as clean with a one-step purifier.
Degradation-aware features and spatial losses guide purification toward preserving object identity, contours, and
geometric and textural cues for downstream 3D generation.
• We construct a synthetic data engine providing paired purification supervision from 3D assets and a benchmark
with 3D ground truth for 3D evaluation. Our method reduces foreground LPIPS by 39.1% and TRELLIS.2 mesh
distance by 61.5% relative to degraded inputs, demonstrating gains in image fidelity and downstream 3D geometry.

3 Method
Gate3DGen is a selective pre-processing model that purifies real-world input images without modifying the downstream
3D generator. It targets images degraded by uneven illumination, blur, and occlusions while encouraging preservation of
geometric and textural cues. Figure 2 summarizes its data engine, router, and one-step purifier.
3.1 Problem Formulation
Let G : X →Y be a pretrained generator mapping a photograph to a 3D representation y ∈Y. A matched clean
reference xc shows a centered, sharp, fully visible subject under uniform lighting, with G(xc) ≈y. An observed
image xo may be clean or degraded. Uneven illumination, blur, and occlusions can violate these assumptions. These
degradations can distort geometry (G(xo)̸ ≈y).

3.3 Degradation-Aware Router
Purifying clean inputs can introduce unwanted changes. To guide purification of images with spatially varying
degradations, we use features extracted from the input image. We therefore design the degradation-aware router Rϕ
based on Qwen2.5-VL (Bai et al., 2025). Trained on Droute, the router predicts whether purification is needed and
extracts continuous degradation-aware features.
Logit-based routing. Given input xo, the router returns (p, h) = Rϕ(xo), where p ∈[0, 1] is the predicted probability
that purification is needed and h denotes continuous degradation-aware features. Rather than using greedy decoding, we
derive the purification probability p directly from the unnormalized logits for the routing decisions PURIFY and KEEP,
denoted by opurify and okeep.

4 Experiments
4.1 Experimental Setup
Benchmark and evaluation protocols. Our benchmark contains 1,082 held-out DTC cases (Dong et al., 2025) with
3D ground truth and clean references sharing the degraded observations’ camera poses. We compare 3D outputs from
degraded, edited, and clean-reference images against ground truth. Our purifier and all editing baselines process every
degraded input. Routing is evaluated separately and does not affect these results.
Baselines. We compare against 7 instruction-guided image editing methods, namely FLUX.1-Kontext (Black Forest
Labs et al., 2025), FireRed-Image-Edit (1.1) (Qiao et al., 2026), Mage-Flow-Edit (Zhang et al., 2026), OmniGen2 (Wu
et al., 2026a), Qwen-Image-Edit (Wu et al., 2025), Step1X-Edit (Liu et al., 2025), and GPT Image-2.5 (Flare) (OpenAI,
2026b). We evaluate downstream 3D quality with Pixal3D (Li et al., 2026) and TRELLIS.2 (Xiang et al., 2026).
Evaluation metrics. For degradation-aware routing, we report PURIFY-class recall, overall accuracy, and H, defined as
their harmonic mean, all expressed as percentages (Table 1). For 2D image fidelity, we report foreground LPIPS, PSNR,
SSIM, Sobel gradient MAE, and foreground mask IoU against the clean reference. For 3D metrics, we report all-surface
Mesh Distance (MD) (Xiang et al., 2026) and F all

4.3 2D Purification Results
Table 2 reports fidelity to clean references. All seven generic editors lower PSNR and SSIM and raise gradient MAE by
17.8%–125.9% relative to unedited inputs. Only FireRed (Qiao et al., 2026) lowers LPIPS, by 0.0004. Degradation
removal thus comes with reduced fidelity to clean references. OmniGen2 (Wu et al., 2026a) also reduces silhouette IoU.
Gate3DGen alone improves all five metrics, lowering LPIPS by 39.1% and gradient MAE by 16.4% while raising IoU
to 0.9758. The gains over the FireRed backbone support the benefit of our purification training (Section 3.4).
Inference efficiency. Gate3DGen achieves the best fidelity across all five metrics with an inference time of 2.3 s,
compared with 15.6–100.8 s for the 6 baselines. This corresponds to a 6.8× speedup over Mage-Flow, the fastest timed
baseline, and a 36.2× speedup over FireRed. Our one-step purifier achieves higher image fidelity and lower inference
time than the compared image editing methods.

4.5 In-the-Wild Results
We evaluate 96 mobile and web photographs without ground-truth meshes using VLM assessment, human preferences,
and FD-DINOv2 (Stein et al., 2023). Qwen3.8-27B serves as the VLM judge (Chen et al., 2024), scoring Identity (object
preservation), Purification (degradation removal), and Quality (asset quality independent of the input). Identity and
Purification average 2D and 3D scores, while Quality averages 3D scores over four views and both frozen generators.
