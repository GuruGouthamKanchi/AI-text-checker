WorldForge V2: Eliciting Camera-Trajectory
Alignment in Video Diffusion Models
Chenxi Song, Yanming Yang, Tong Zhao, Ruibo Li,
Joey Tianyi Zhou, Senior Member, IEEE, and Chi Zhang, Member, IEEE
Abstract—Video diffusion models have learned rich 3D/4D
priors, making them promising for scene generation and video
re-rendering. However, their use in these spatial tasks is limited
by imprecise camera control, spatial and temporal inconsis-
tencies, and the entanglement of camera motion with scene
dynamics. Existing approaches, such as task-specific fine-tuning
and post-process warping, often introduce visual artifacts, gen-
eralize poorly, or incur high computational costs. We propose
WorldForge V2, which aims to achieve accurate camera control
while preserving the generative capabilities of pretrained video
models. We first develop a training-free guidance framework
using warped references to iteratively refine generation along
a target camera path. Optical-flow analysis identifies motion-
related latent channels for selective guidance, helping separate
motion control from appearance. A dual-path strategy further
compares guided and unguided predictions to correct artifacts
from imperfect references. Together, these components guide
viewpoint changes while preserving scene appearance and object
motion. To reduce refinement costs, v-cache approximates the
correction using the preceding denoising prediction, avoiding an
additional model evaluation. We further extend motion local-
ization from latent channels to network blocks and introduce
Trajectory LoRA, which concentrates adaptation on blocks most
responsive to trajectory guidance. A two-stage training strategy
teaches these adapters to denoise the states encountered during
guided generation, using limited supervision effectively. This
enables reliable camera control with very little training data,
with a single training scene already supporting generalization
to unseen scenes. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations across
multiple datasets show that our method achieves state-of-the-art
generation quality and trajectory accuracy in camera-controlled
video generation.
Index Terms—Camera control, video diffusion models, 3D/4D
generation.

To realize such control, existing approaches broadly follow
two directions. One direction adapts VDMs for camera control
through additional trainable modules, pose-aware conditioning
interfaces, or model fine-tuning [5], [12]–[16]. These meth-
ods provide explicit camera control but often require large-
scale trajectory–video supervision or careful fine-tuning. With
limited supervision, such adaptation may weaken the visual
and temporal priors of the pretrained model. Recent work
has therefore explored training-free alternatives that keep the
pretrained VDM fixed and inject trajectory cues only during
inference [6], [17], including the zero-shot variant of Warp-as-
History [18]. A representative route is warping-and-repainting,
which warps already observed content along the target camera
path and uses the pretrained video model to synthesize newly
revealed regions. This route enables direct camera control at
inference time while preserving the pretrained model, making
it well suited to downstream spatial-intelligence tasks.
Despite these advantages, warping-and-repainting can intro-
duce a denoising-state mismatch during diffusion sampling.
When warped visible regions are directly combined with
model-generated regions in a noisy latent, the two parts
may correspond to different noise states. The warped regions
are associated with externally sampled noise, whereas the

We refer to this mismatch as a denoising-state domain gap.
It can lead to boundary discontinuities, geometric artifacts,

To address this problem, we propose WorldForge V2, a
camera-trajectory-control framework that helps VDMs use
imperfect warped trajectory signals more effectively while
preserving generation quality. WorldForge V2 follows the
warping-and-repainting route. First, it addresses the incom-
patibility introduced by warped-reference fusion: direct fusion
in noisy latents mixes externally noised warped regions with
generated regions following the model’s current denoising
state. WorldForge V2 reduces this mismatch by fusing the
warped reference into the model-predicted clean estimate and
mapping the fused result back to the current denoising state,
enabling training-free trajectory-aligned video generation. Sec-
ond, clean-estimate fusion provides a reliable way to inject
warped references into the denoising process by reducing
noisy-state mismatch. To further improve its sampling effi-
ciency, we introduce v-cache, which approximates the clean-
estimate correction directly in noisy latents. In this way,
warped-reference guidance can retain the benefit of clean-
estimate fusion while reducing the extra sampling overhead.
Finally, full-model fine-tuning and large trainable control mod-
ules involve optimizing a large number of parameters, making
it difficult to use limited training data efficiently. To address
this problem, we build on our pipeline and further investigate
an optimization-based approach to camera-trajectory control.
Since different layers in VDMs specialize in different func-
tions, we extend the motion-channel localization used in FLF
to model adaptation. We identify trajectory-sensitive blocks
and apply LoRA only to these blocks. This selective adaptation
provides a data-efficient alternative to broad model tuning.
First, we address the denoising-state incompatibility caused
by warped-reference fusion. In direct noisy-space fusion, the
warped visible regions and the model-generated regions may
follow different denoising states, causing geometric artifacts,
boundary discontinuities, and noisy patches. To mitigate this
mismatch, Intra-Step Recursive Refinement (IRR) fuses the
warped reference with the model-predicted clean estimate
under the visibility mask and then re-noises the fused estimate
to the current timestep. This fuse-then-renoise process asso-
ciates all regions with the same Gaussian noise realization,
thereby reducing their denoising-state gap. Camera-trajectory
control also faces a typical motion–appearance disentangle-
ment problem, as enforcing camera motion may inadvertently
alter object and scene appearance. To address this problem,
Flow-Gated Latent Fusion (FLF) uses optical-flow cues to
locate channels that are highly correlated with motion and
injects trajectory guidance only into these channels, thereby
reducing appearance distortion. Finally, artifacts or misalign-
ment in the warped reference may degrade the quality of the
generated video. To mitigate this issue, we propose Dual-
Path Self-Corrective Guidance (DSG). Inspired by classifier-
free guidance [19], DSG compares the trajectory-guided and
unguided denoising directions. Their difference captures the
update introduced by trajectory guidance, which DSG uses to
suppress unreliable deviations while retaining useful trajectory
constraints. Together, IRR, FLF, and DSG enable training-
free trajectory-aligned video generation while reducing the
adverse effects of incompatible denoising states and unreliable
reference signals.
Second, the fuse-then-renoise process in IRR provides an
effective way to reduce the denoising-state gap introduced by
warped-reference fusion. We further improve the efficiency of
this process with v-cache. Our key observation is that the cor-
rection induced by clean-estimate fusion can be approximated
directly in noisy latent space from a denoising prediction
already available during sampling. Based on this observation,
v-cache applies the approximated correction during warped-
reference fusion, allowing trajectory guidance to remain on
the original denoising path. This avoids an additional model
evaluation and reduces inference latency.
Finally, we further develop an optimization-based exten-
sion of our pipeline for camera-trajectory control. Existing
optimization-based methods often fine-tune the full model
or introduce large trainable control modules, making ineffi-
cient use of limited supervision. Since camera control mainly
depends on motion-related representations, we extend the
motion-channel localization idea of FLF to model adaptation.
Leveraging the functional specialization of different VDM lay-
ers [20], we identify trajectory-sensitive blocks, apply LoRA
only to these blocks, and train the adapters with a two-stage
optimization strategy tailored to our pipeline. This selective
adaptation provides a data-efficient alternative to broad model
tuning. In our experiments, we find that training data from
a single scene is sufficient to enable effective trajectory
following.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• To address denoising-state mismatch, motion–appearance
entanglement, and unreliable warped references, we de-
velop a training-free guidance pipeline with IRR, FLF,
and DSG, enabling trajectory-aligned video generation
with fewer artifacts and less appearance distortion.
• To retain the benefit of clean-estimate correction with
lower sampling cost, we introduce v-cache, which ap-
proximates the correction in noisy latent space and avoids
an additional model evaluation, thereby reducing infer-
ence latency.
• To avoid data-inefficient full-model tuning, we identify
trajectory-sensitive blocks, adapt only these blocks with
LoRA, and optimize them using a two-stage strategy
tailored to our pipeline; training data from a single scene
is sufficient to enable effective trajectory following.
• Comparative experiments on multiple public datasets
demonstrate accurate camera-path control and high-
quality video generation.

II. RELATED WORK
We review four lines of work closely related to WorldForge
V2: video diffusion priors for spatial generation, camera
control in video diffusion models, guidance and correction
strategies in generative models, and parameter-efficient adap-
tation for camera control.
A. Video Diffusion Priors for Spatial Generation
Video diffusion models (VDMs) learn rich priors of real-
world appearance, motion, temporal continuity, and viewpoint
change from large-scale video data [1]–[3], [22]. Recent
studies have transferred these priors to spatial generation tasks,
including 3D/4D scene generation, novel-view video synthesis,
and world modeling [4]–[6], [8]–[10], [14], [23]–[30]. This
scene-level use of video priors complements explicit scene
reconstruction based on NeRFs, Gaussian splatting, multi-view
and light-field synthesis [31]–[37], as well as scene generation
from panoramic images [38] and object-centric 3D generation
based on diffusion models [39]–[43]. While reconstruction
methods recover scene structure from observed views, VDMs
can use their learned priors to generate plausible content
beyond the observed regions. However, VDM pretraining does
not explicitly align camera trajectories with scene content. As
a result, these models can generate high-quality videos but
cannot reliably follow a prescribed camera path, motivating
growing research on trajectory-controlled video generation.
WorldForge V2 combines warped geometric evidence with the
generative prior of a pretrained VDM to control viewpoint
changes.
B. Camera Control in Video Diffusion Models
Existing camera-control methods broadly follow two di-
rections. Training-based methods introduce pose-aware condi-
tioning interfaces, additional control modules, or model fine-
tuning to learn camera-controlled generation [5], [12]–[16],
[44], [45]. These methods provide explicit camera control
but usually require substantial trajectory–video supervision;
when training data are limited, model adaptation may weaken
the visual and temporal priors learned during pretraining.
Recent work has therefore increasingly explored training-free
inference-time control and lightweight adaptation, which pre-
serve most or all of the pretrained model and inject trajectory
cues through initial noise, latent transformations, rendered
references, or history frames [6], [8], [17], [18], [46]–[48].
Across training-based and training-free methods, warping-and-
repainting warps observed content along the target camera path
and uses a VDM to generate newly revealed regions [49],
[50]. This route provides explicit geometric correspondence
and integrates naturally with depth estimation, point-based
rendering, and visibility masks. WorldForge V2 adopts this
route and studies how warped references can be used more
effectively for camera-trajectory control while reducing the
artifacts introduced during denoising. Related motion-control
work also uses entity-specific 3D trajectories to control object
motion [51].
C. Guidance and Correction Strategies in Generative Models
Guidance methods steer diffusion or flow models to-
ward desired outputs by modifying their sampling directions.
Classifier-free guidance (CFG) combines conditional and un-
conditional predictions to improve alignment with seman-
tic conditions [19]. Subsequent methods further adjust the
guidance direction through negative or auxiliary branches,
differential predictions, adaptive rescaling, resampling, and
iterative repainting [52]–[58]. In 3D/4D generation, guidance
is also widely used to enforce viewpoint consistency from
rendered or warped observations [6], [8], [17], [59], [60].

However, conventional guidance does not directly address the
artifacts introduced by noisy warped references. WorldForge
V2 explores correction strategies for warping-guided gener-
ation, including correcting warped-reference fusion in noisy
latent space and using the difference between guided and
unguided predictions to refine the denoising process. Together,
these strategies improve the quality of trajectory-controlled
videos generated under warping guidance.
D. Parameter-Efficient Adaptation for Camera Control
Model adaptation commonly uses trainable control branches
or parameter-efficient fine-tuning. ControlNet and T2I-Adapter
introduce lightweight conditioning modules, while LoRA
adapts pretrained models through low-rank parameter up-
dates [61]–[63]. These lightweight adaptation strategies have
also been applied to motion learning, sparse-view synthe-
sis, and trajectory-controlled generation with limited training
data [18], [45], [64], [65]. Related studies further show that
motion- and camera-related representations are concentrated
in particular latent channels or network layers [20], [66].
Based on these findings, WorldForge V2 uses optical-flow
cues in FLF to localize motion-related latent channels and
reduce appearance interference. We further extend this idea
to model adaptation by identifying trajectory-sensitive blocks
and applying lightweight LoRA only to these blocks, enabling
effective trajectory alignment with very limited training data.
III. METHOD
WorldForge V2 (Fig. 2) aims to control camera trajectories
while preserving the video priors of pretrained generation
models. Given a source image or video and a target camera
path, geometric warping first produces a trajectory reference
and its visibility mask. WorldForge V2 then uses these sig-
nals to guide denoising and generate videos that follow the
target path. Sec. III-A introduces the required notation and
background. Sec. III-B presents the training-free guidance
pipeline: IRR reduces the denoising-state gap through clean-
estimate fusion, FLF restricts trajectory injection to motion-
related latent channels, and DSG corrects the guided pre-
diction using the model’s original denoising path. Sec. III-C
derives a noisy-latent correction that reduces the denoising-
state gap with fewer model evaluations. Finally, Sec. III-D
identifies trajectory-sensitive network blocks and adapts them
with LoRA using limited supervision, enabling data-efficient
trajectory control for VDMs.
A. Preliminaries
We introduce the notation and background required by
WorldForge V2, including flow-based denoising, sampling-
time guidance, low-rank adaptation, and geometry-based tra-
jectory guidance.

vided in the supplementary material.

B. Inference-Time Trajectory Guidance
Geometric warping explicitly indicates how observed con-
tent should move along the target camera path. However,
directly inserting the noised reference mixes the externally
re-noised visible region with the model-generated region,
producing a denoising-state gap that can cause noisy patches
and structural errors. We therefore inject the reference through
the model-predicted clean estimate and re-noise the fused
result with a unified noise sample. We further restrict guidance
to motion-related channels and calibrate the guided prediction
against the model’s original denoising path. Together, these
designs enable training-free trajectory control while preserving
visual quality.
Intra-Step Recursive Refinement (IRR). Direct noisy-latent

region while retaining the model’s current denoising state
elsewhere. To avoid directly combining these incompatible
states, IRR first fuses the warped reference in the model-
predicted clean space and then re-noises the fused result with
one Gaussian noise sample.
