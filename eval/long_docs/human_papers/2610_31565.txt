ABSTRACT
Generating 360◦panoramas requires both local perspective fidelity and global
spherical continuity. Existing representations face a trade-off: equirectangular
projection (ERP) enables seamless panorama modeling but suffers from severe
distortion and a domain gap to pre-trained image generators, whereas cubemaps
preserve perspective geometry but often produce inconsistent faces and visible
seams.
We present SQUARING-THE-SPHERE, a panoramic image generation
framework built on a novel representation CubeAtlas, which unfolds all cube-
map faces into a single compact canvas. By arranging orientation-aligned, rotated
polar regions alongside the equatorial faces, this representation preserves the per-
spective geometry of cubemaps while organizing the cross-face relationships re-
quired for seamless panorama assembly. It further enables a pretrained diffusion
transformer to model the complete panorama jointly in a single pass, without al-
tering the backbone architecture or positional encoding. While CubeAtlas has a
token count comparable to ERP, its structured layout makes many dense attention
interactions unnecessary. We therefore introduce a layout-aware sparse attention
mask that preserves the interactions required for panorama continuity while re-
moving geometrically irrelevant token pairs. As a result, CubeAtlas achieves bet-
ter quality than ERP modeling with lower attention computation. Furthermore,
we introduce a training-free super-resolution technique and a fusion scheme that
enhance fine details in distortion-free perspective space before achieving the fi-
nal ERP panorama. Experiments on various benchmarks demonstrate that our
method improves geometric fidelity, cross-view consistency, and high-resolution
visual quality over previous methods.

INTRODUCTION
360◦panoramas serve as a fundamental visual medium for a broad range of immersive applications,
including virtual and augmented reality, gaming, telepresence, and 3D world modeling (Hunyuan-
World et al., 2025; HY-World Team et al., 2026; World Labs, 2025; Ji et al., 2026; Jia et al., 2026).
Recent advances in image generative models have substantially improved the realism, diversity, and
controllability of perspective image synthesis (Esser et al., 2024; Black Forest Labs, 2024; Wu et al.,
2025; Cao et al., 2025). However, how to transfer the powerful generative priors learned in perspec-
tive space to panorama generation remains an open challenge, as a panorama must simultaneously
preserve local perspective fidelity and maintain globally coherent, seamless content over the entire
sphere (Figure 1).
In this paper, we identify the key challenge as selecting a panoramic representation compatible with
pre-trained image priors. The two most common choices are equirectangular projection (ERP) and
cubemaps. ERP represents the scene on a single equirectangular canvas, facilitating joint model-
ing of the entire panorama (Zhang et al., 2024; Feng et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2026; Huang et al.,
2026). However, its non-uniform spherical projection severely stretches polar regions, introduces
geometric distortion, and departs from the perspective-image distribution learned by modern gen-
erators (Liang et al., 2026). Cubemaps avoid ERP’s polar stretching by decomposing the sphere
into six perspective faces (Tang et al., 2023; Kalischek et al., 2025). Although methods such as
CubeDiff jointly model these faces through cross-face attention, preserving spatial continuity across
face boundaries remains challenging. Therefore, unifying ERP’s global coherence with cubemaps’
perspective fidelity remains a central problem for high-quality panorama generation.

To address this trade-off, we propose SQUARING-THE-SPHERE, a framework built on a new
panorama representation termed CubeAtlas. Rather than generating an ERP image or six indepen-
dent cube faces, SQUARING-THE-SPHERE unfolds the complete cubemaps into a single image-like
grid and enables a diffusion transformer (DiT) (Peebles & Xie, 2023) to model the entire panorama
jointly in one forward pass, as illustrated by the CubeAtlas layout in the upper part of Figure 1. By
combining half-polar rotation and packing for seam-aware yet compact face alignment with layout-
aware sparse attention for efficient token interactions, SQUARING-THE-SPHERE promotes cross-
view continuity without the cost of dense global attention. Crucially, our representation is compat-
ible with existing DiT-based image generators without modifying their backbone architectures or
positional encodings, thereby inheriting large-scale 2D image priors with minimal adaptation.
Concretely, CubeAtlas re-arranges the six cubemap faces into a compact three-band, four-column
layout, as detailed in Figure 2. For each equatorial orientation, it retains only the equator-adjacent
half of the upper and lower faces, rotated to align with the corresponding equatorial view. This half-
polar layout retains the local context needed to connect each polar region to its neighboring equato-
rial faces while avoiding redundant polar tokens; complementary rotated copies are later combined
to reconstruct the complete polar faces. Polar rotation aligns polar content with the correspond-
ing equatorial views, turning otherwise discontinuous cross-face relationships into locally organized
neighborhoods within the grid, thereby facilitating seamless grid-to-ERP assembly. We further in-
troduce layout-aware sparse attention that preserves the intra-face and continuity-related interactions
required for panorama generation while masking irrelevant token pairs. Specifically, full attention
is retained within each latitude band for horizontal consistency, while cross-latitude communication
is mediated solely by the equatorial band: upper and lower regions never interact directly, and each
equatorial region attends only to the polar regions on the same left–right or front–back axis. Finally,
we reproject the ERP into overlapping perspective cube views and apply a training-free 4× super-
resolution model (Lu et al., 2026a) to each view. Low-frequency correction preserves coherent color
and illumination in the final ERP, while linear blending of overlaps solves discontinuities.

RELATED WORK
360◦Panorama Generation. Existing panorama generators are largely distinguished by how they
represent the sphere (Xia et al., 2026). ERP-based methods synthesize a single canvas, which facili-
tates global generation (Chen et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2024; Feng et al., 2023), but polar stretching
creates a mismatch with the perspective-image distribution of pretrained generators. Panorama-
specific adaptations address this mismatch through seam-aware processing (Wu et al., 2026; Kang
et al., 2026; Lu et al., 2026b) or hybrid perspective–panorama training (Feng et al., 2026). Cubemap-
based methods instead preserve perspective geometry while explicitly adapting the model to commu-
nicate across separate faces. CubeDiff (Kalischek et al., 2025) jointly denoises six faces through in-
flated attention and augments their latents with spherical UV coordinates, while DreamCube (Huang
et al., 2025) uses spherical coordinates and synchronizes attention to improve boundary consistency.
Although both support joint generation within a single forward pass, they retain separate face-wise
spatial grids and introduce panorama-specific positional signals or operators to mitigate cross-face
seams. Our method instead packs orientation-aligned cubemap faces into a single CubeAtlas canvas,
turning cross-face adjacencies into local 2D neighborhoods and enabling seam-consistent panorama
generation with the pretrained DiT’s native positional encoding and backbone structure.
Image-to-Panorama Generation. Image-to-panorama methods extend a narrow-field perspective
image into a complete environment. Earlier diffusion-based approaches typically formulate the task
as panoramic outpainting, using camera-aware projection and inpainting to align the conditioning
image with an ERP target (Wu et al., 2024). More recently, 360Anything (Wu et al., 2026) and HY-
Pano 2.0 (HY-World Team et al., 2026) learn the perspective-to-ERP mapping directly from data in
a geometry-free manner, without explicit camera metadata or camera calibration. SQUARING-THE-
SPHERE shares this geometry-free formulation: it consumes the conditioning image as-is, without
camera calibration, explicit input projection, or geometric inpainting. Unlike methods that directly

Priors from Generative Image Models.
Large-scale diffusion and flow-based image genera-
tors (Esser et al., 2024; Black Forest Labs, 2024; Wu et al., 2025) provide strong priors for high-
fidelity, controllable perspective-image synthesis. Directly transferring these planar priors to ERP
panoramas is nevertheless non-trivial: their standard spatial representations do not encode spherical
periodicity or polar convergence. Recent training-free adaptations, e.g., SpheRoPE (Hirschorn et al.,
2026) and SphereDiff (Park et al., 2026), modify positional encoding or use spherical projection at
inference time to inject these spherical constraints into frozen generators (Hayakawa et al., 2026).
Such methods demonstrate the useful panoramic priors already present in foundation models, but
their generation capability must still be induced at inference time rather than learned in a panorama-
specific representation. SQUARING-THE-SPHERE instead fine-tunes a pre-trained generative DiT
on CubeAtlas formulations, preserving its perspective-image prior while learning joint cross-face
generation in a representation designed for panoramic continuity.

METHOD
We introduce SQUARING-THE-SPHERE, an effective approach for generating high-quality, seamless
360◦panoramas, as provided in Figure 3. We first motivate the CubeAtlas layout by reviewing
common panorama representations in Section 3.1. Then we describe model details in Section 3.2.
3.1
PANORAMIC REPRESENTATION
A panorama captures a complete 360◦×180◦view of a scene from a fixed viewpoint, and the choice
of how to lay this view out on a flat image plane has a decisive effect on generation quality.
3.1.1
PREVIOUS REPRESENTATIONS
Equirectangular Projection (ERP). ERP maps longitude and latitude onto the horizontal and ver-
tical axes of a 2 : 1 rectangular image, providing a simple and compact representation of the full
sphere. However, its sampling becomes increasingly non-uniform toward the poles, causing se-
vere horizontal stretching and departing from the perspective-image domain of pretrained diffusion
models. This mismatch makes it difficult to generate faithful geometry in polar regions. Moreover,
spherical continuity is split across the left and right image boundaries, requiring explicit handling of
horizontal wrap-around.
Cubemap. A cubemap projects the sphere onto six perspective faces, each with a 90◦Field-of-View
(FoV). It avoids the severe polar stretching of ERP and better aligns with the perspective-image

domain of pretrained diffusion models. However, content that is continuous on the sphere is split
across separate face boundaries. Without effective cross-face modeling, generated faces can become
inconsistent in appearance, semantics, and geometry, resulting in visible seams along cube edges.
3.1.2
CUBEATLAS
To unify both advantages, we propose a new representation that retains the perspective-face geome-
try of cubemaps while organizing their cross-face relationships into a single image-like canvas. Let
S denote the side length of each cube face in latent space. We denote the six standard cubemap
orientations by Ω= {L, F, R, B, U, D}, corresponding to left, front, right, back, up, and down. The
four equatorial orientations (L, F, R, B) are placed side by side in the central band. Jointly denoising
this complete band in one forward pass allows the model to learn the horizontal continuity directly.
The more challenging boundaries are vertical. In a conventional cubemap-cross representation, only
the front face is directly adjacent to the upper and lower faces; the left, right, and back faces are spa-
tially separated from their corresponding polar neighborhoods and receive incompatible positional
relationships. CubeAtlas therefore creates an in-plane-rotated copy of each polar face for every
equatorial orientation, such that its equator-adjacent half is vertically aligned with the correspond-
ing equatorial face. We denote these upper half-regions by UL, UF, UR, UB, while the lower ones
are DL, DF, DR, DB. The core layout of CubeAtlas can be presented as:

3.2
MODEL DESIGN
Geometry-Free Panorama Generation.
We build SQUARING-THE-SPHERE on a pre-trained
image-editing DiT. The conditioning image is consumed directly: we neither estimate nor provide
camera intrinsics, camera pose, or an explicit projection of the input onto the sphere. Instead, the
model learns from data how to place the input content on the 360◦CubeAtlas layout and synthesize
its surrounding context.
The conditioning image follows the standard dual-path design. It is jointly encoded with the text by
the frozen multimodal encoder (Bai et al., 2025) for semantic context, and independently encoded
by the frozen VAE into clean visual condition tokens. These condition tokens are concatenated with
noisy CubeAtlas target tokens, and the DiT operates on the joint text, target, and condition sequence.
We fully fine-tune the DiT using the standard linear flow-matching objective, which predicts ϵ −x0
from xσ = (1 −σ)x0 + σϵ, with σ sampled from a logit-normal distribution. We make no changes
to the DiT architecture or its rotary position embeddings. Consequently, SQUARING-THE-SPHERE
preserves the strong perspective-image prior of the base model while adapting it to joint panorama
generation in CubeAtlas space.
Layout-Aware Sparse Attention.
Although the CubeAtlas canvas has a token count comparable
to that of ERP, dense attention over all its regions is not always necessary to maintain panoramic
consistency. A key advantage of CubeAtlas is that it makes geometrically adjacent faces locally
connected while retaining a structured decomposition of the panorama. We exploit this property with
a layout-aware sparse attention mask over target-to-target interactions; text and condition tokens
remain globally connected to every target token.
Specifically, we partition the target into upper, equatorial, and lower bands. We retain full attention
within each band to preserve horizontal consistency, but block direct interactions between the up-
per and lower polar bands. For cross-latitude communication, each equatorial face attends only to
the polar half-regions aligned with its axis. For example, L attends to {UL, UR, DL, DR}, whose
complementary halves reconstruct its neighboring upper and lower faces. Conversely, a polar query
attends to all equatorial keys, allowing the equatorial band to serve as the cross-latitude bridge. Over-
all, the layout-aware mask retains 75% of the target-to-target attention connectivity of dense atten-
tion, as illustrated in Figure 3, while text and image-conditioning tokens remain globally connected
to all target tokens. Blocking direct attention between some regions does not prevent information
exchange: features can still propagate through the equatorial band across successive DiT layers.
The mask therefore structures cross-region communication rather than separating the panorama into
independently modeled regions. Our experiments further show that this structured sparsity supports
cross-view consistency while improving efficiency.
Panoramic Super-Resolution.
To obtain high-resolution panoramas without heavy training, we
apply a frozen 4× Pixel Diffusion Decoder (PiD) (Lu et al., 2026a) in perspective space. Specif-
ically, the fused ERP is reprojected into six overlapping cubemap views with an enlarged FoV of
92.79◦, which are independently enhanced by PiD and mapped back to the ERP domain. This pro-
cess upsamples the base panorama by 4× along each spatial dimension, producing 10k ERP panora-
mas. We linearly blend overlapping regions outside the FoV range of 90◦to suppress cross-view
discontinuities. Since generative super-resolution may introduce low-frequency shifts, we further

EXPERIMENTS
Implementation Details.
We initialize SQUARING-THE-SPHERE from Wu et al. (2025). The
VAE and Qwen2.5-VL condition encoder are kept frozen, while the denoising Transformer is fully
fine-tuned for 8,000 steps with a global batch size of 64 and a learning rate of 10−5. We apply Flex-
Attention (Guessous et al., 2024) for the sparse attention implementation. Each cubemap face in
CubeAtlas has a resolution of 640 × 640, corresponding to a fused ERP panorama of 1280 × 2560.
The super-resolution stage further upsamples the panorama by 4× along each spatial dimension,
producing a 5120 × 10240 panorama with a horizontal resolution of 10K. Our evaluation bench-
mark consists of 100 self-collected images, together with 100, 100, and 118 panoramas from the
RealSee3D (Li et al., 2026), Laval-Indoor (Gardner et al., 2017), and SUN360 (Xiao et al., 2012)
test sets, respectively. We compare against CubeDiff (Kalischek et al., 2025), DreamCube (Huang
et al., 2025), HunyuanPano (HunyuanWorld et al., 2025), and HY-Pano-2.0 (HY-World Team et al.,
2026) using their official inference configurations. Unless otherwise specified, all comparisons are
conducted on the base-resolution outputs without super-resolution refinement, ensuring a fair com-
parison across methods. Additional datasets and evaluation details are provided in Section B.
4.1
MAIN RESULTS
Quantitative Results.
Table 1 compares SQUARING-THE-SPHERE with representative panorama
generation systems. The metrics cover distributional fidelity (FID, KID, and CLIP-FID), ERP and
cubemap boundary continuity (SeamERP, SeamCube), equatorial and polar perceptual and geometric
quality (Eq-* and Pole-*), and prompt adherence (TextAdh); full definitions are provided in Sec-
tion D. SQUARING-THE-SPHERE achieves the lowest SeamCube on all four benchmarks, reducing
the cube-edge error by 63–78% relative to CubeDiff. These results support the effectiveness of gen-
erating a single canvas directly to eliminate cross-face discontinuities. Note that at SeamERP values
around 0.01, further numerical differences may not translate into perceptible differences in bound-
ary seams. We therefore interpret this metric together with the qualitative comparisons. SQUARING-
THE-SPHERE also leads Eq-GI and Pole-GI on every benchmark and ranks first or second in Pole-IQ,
demonstrating improved equatorial and polar geometry. Meanwhile, it achieves the best CLIP-FID
on all three public benchmarks, leads FID and KID on RealSee3D and FID on Laval-Indoor, and ob-
tains the highest TextAdh. These results confirm that SQUARING-THE-SPHERE improves spherical
structure without sacrificing visual realism or textual consistency.
