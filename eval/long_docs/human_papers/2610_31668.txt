Abstract
Video foundation models have advanced rapidly in understanding and generating visual
sequences, while video world models seek to capture the underlying dynamics of the world
they depict. Despite their different objectives, both concern the relationship between video
observations and an evolving world. This survey adopts world modeling as a unifying lens for
examining these developments. Viewing an observed video as a partial, lossy, and temporally
ordered projection of a latent temporal world, we organize the literature around four com-
plementary operations:
Y reading infers world properties from observed video;
Ò writing
synthesizes video from specified or learned world conditions;
T sharing integrates understand-
ing and generation through common modeling mechanisms; and
 interacting predicts and
updates world states under actions and evolving observations. This perspective distinguishes
temporal coherence in observed or generated sequences from consistency in the underlying
world state. Across tasks, representations, models, datasets, and benchmarks, we trace a
shift in emphasis from clip-level recognition and perceptual fidelity toward persistent state,
controllable evolution, physical and causal validity, and evaluation grounded in interaction
and downstream decisions, and we distill the open challenges that follow.
