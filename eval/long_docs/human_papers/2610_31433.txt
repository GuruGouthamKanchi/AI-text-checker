Drift Says When, Budget Says How Much:
Rate-Controlled KV Re-selection for Long-Form Multimodal Generation
Bin Hu
Tsinghua University
Yingbo Xiao
The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen
Abstract
KV cache compression for multimodal LLMs
usually selects the visual tokens to keep once,
at prefill, and reuses that selection for the whole
generation.
We show that this one-shot se-
lection is severely limited on long-form gen-
eration: on detailed image description, even
an oracle that selects with the attention of the
uncompressed answer reaches only 0.44–0.52
ROUGE-L agreement with the uncompressed
output at 32 retained prefix tokens per head,
because the evidence the model reads moves as
the description unfolds. Re-selecting whenever
attention drifts out of the kept set is the natural
remedy, but we find that a fixed drift threshold
fails systematically: drift magnitudes are not
comparable across samples, so low-drift sam-
ples are almost never refreshed although they
benefit the most. We propose Rate-Controlled
Refresh (RCR), which lets drift decide when
and where to re-select while a per-sample con-
troller regulates how often, combined with a
low-rank drift detector, candidate-restricted re-
selection, and a decision that stays on the GPU.
With one setting for all models and tasks, RCR
outperforms the per-step retrieval method In-
finiGen by 1.6–3.8 ROUGE-L points on five
of six model–task settings with no significant
difference on the sixth, while staying within
1.7 points of exact per-step re-selection, with
comparable estimated data movement per step
and a 4× smaller GPU-resident detector. On
DetailCaps, drift-chosen timing beats random
timing at the same refresh rate and periodic
timing even at a higher rate, by 2.0–5.0 points;
the advantage over InfiniGen is largest at tight
budgets and shrinks as the budget grows. On
pre-registered video description, where drift is
homogeneous across samples, RCR is on par
with InfiniGen on Qwen2-VL and exceeds it on
Qwen3-VL but no longer beats a fixed thresh-
old at the nearest tested rates, consistent with
the diagnosis; object-level evaluation shows
that RCR achieves higher object F1 against the
uncompressed output than InfiniGen on two of
three models.

Introduction
The KV cache of a multimodal large language
model (MLLM) is dominated by visual tokens: in
our long-form benchmarks the prefix holds 640–
2,300 tokens per sample, almost all of them im-
age patches. Prefill-time eviction methods such as
SnapKV (Li et al., 2024), PyramidKV (Cai et al.,
2024), Ada-KV (Feng et al., 2025) and the mul-
timodal BACON (Chen et al., 2026) score prefix
tokens with the attention of an observation win-
dow and keep only the top few. On visual question
answering one-shot selection can be close to loss-
less: with 64 KV entries per head, an oracle that
scores with the true answer’s attention matches the
uncompressed model on TextVQA and DocVQA.
Long-form generation is different. On detailed
captioning (DetailCaps; Dong et al., 2024) bet-
ter scoring helps but cannot close the gap: even
the answer-attention oracle keeps only 0.44–0.52
ROUGE-L agreement with the uncompressed out-
put (§3). The reason is that attention moves: as the
model describes the skateboarder, then the ramp,
then the trash bin (Figure 1), the evidence it needs
leaves the set chosen at prefill. With 32 slots per
head, even an oracle-scored one-shot selection falls
well short.
A natural remedy is to keep the full KV off the
GPU and re-select whenever the attention mass
on evicted tokens, which we call drift, exceeds
a threshold. This already beats periodic refresh
at equal refresh rates (Appendix D), yet it trails
InfiniGen (Lee et al., 2024), which re-selects at
every step, by 1.9 points on Qwen2-VL DetailCaps.
A per-sample analysis (§4) shows where the gap
comes from: on high-drift samples the threshold
rule already matches exact per-step re-selection,
but on low-drift samples it refreshes 1% of the time
while exact re-selection would gain 30 points over

We therefore decouple the two decisions. RCR
keeps drift as the signal for when and in which
layer to re-select, but adapts the threshold online
per sample so that a target fraction ρ of layer-steps
refresh. Three further components make refreshing
accurate and cheap: drift is estimated from 8 SVD-
rotated key channels, re-selection scores exactly
only the detector’s top candidates, and the trigger
decision never leaves the GPU. Our contributions
are:
• We quantify the limits of one-shot selection
on long-form multimodal generation across
three MLLMs (§3).
• We identify why fixed drift thresholds fail: the
gap to per-step retrieval comes almost entirely
from low-drift samples (§4); on video, where
almost no sample is starved, the advantage of
rate control over a fixed threshold disappears,
consistent with this diagnosis.
• We propose RCR, which separates when
(drift) from how much (a per-sample rate bud-
get). One configuration outperforms Infini-
Gen on five of six model–task settings with no
significant difference on the sixth, at compa-
rable CPU traffic; with everything else fixed,
drift-chosen timing beats random timing at the
same rate and periodic timing even at a higher
rate by 2.0–5.0 points (§6).
• We follow a develop-then-confirm protocol:
components were chosen on a 100-sample de-
velopment split and confirmed on held-out
data under a pre-registered decision rule (Ap-
pendix A).

Related Work
One-shot KV eviction.
H2O (Zhang et al., 2023)
and Scissorhands (Liu et al., 2023) keep heavy hit-
ters under a persistence-of-importance assumption;
SnapKV (Li et al., 2024) votes with an observation
window, and PyramidKV (Cai et al., 2024), Ada-
KV (Feng et al., 2025), HeadKV (Fu et al., 2025)
and CAKE (Qin et al., 2025) redistribute budgets
across layers and heads. Multimodal variants ex-
ploit modality structure (Wan et al., 2024; Liu et al.,
2024c; Tu et al., 2025; Wan et al., 2025; Huang
et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a; Liu
et al., 2026); BACON (Chen et al., 2026) is a strong
low-budget selector that we use at prefill. Look-
ahead methods (Wang et al., 2025b; Galim et al.,
2026; Ahn et al., 2026) estimate future queries be-
fore a single eviction. All of them select once; we
show this limits long-form fidelity even with oracle
scores. Visual token reduction prunes or merges
image tokens inside the model (Chen et al., 2024a;
Zhang et al., 2025b; Xing et al., 2025; Yang et al.,
2025b), with dynamic or recoverable variants for
generation (Liang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2026);
these change the computation itself and are comple-
mentary to KV selection. For long outputs, several
methods keep updating a compressed cache as text
is generated (Wu et al., 2025; Ghadia et al., 2025;
Liao et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025a; Cai et al.,
2025); RCR instead re-selects among all prefix to-
kens from a full copy, so visual evidence evicted
earlier can return.
Decode-time retrieval.
Another line keeps the
full KV (often offloaded) and retrieves a subset
at every step: Quest (Tang et al., 2024) bounds
page scores with channel-wise key extrema, Ark-
Vale (Chen et al., 2024b) recalls pages by bounding-
sphere digests, and InfiniGen (Lee et al., 2024)
speculates attention from a few SVD-skewed key
channels; related designs use low-rank keys, ap-
proximate nearest neighbours or hashing (Ribar
et al., 2024; Singhania et al., 2024; Liu et al.,
2024a; Chen et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025a; Jie
et al., 2025), share selections across layers (Yang
et al., 2025a; Hao et al., 2025), or filter coarse-to-
fine (Behnam et al., 2025). Twilight (Lin et al.,
2025) replaces fixed top-k budgets with top-p prun-
ing, and ParisKV (Qi et al., 2026) makes retrieval
robust to query drift; both target per-step sparse
attention rather than deciding when to re-select.
RCR borrows InfiniGen’s skewing for its detector
and coarse-to-fine filtering for re-selection, but re-
trieves only on a budgeted fraction of layer-steps
chosen by drift.
Deciding when to recompute.
ReSA (Sun
et al., 2025b), Tactic (Zhu et al., 2025) and Re-
freshKV (Xu et al., 2024) recompute at fixed in-
tervals; dynamic variants trigger on query sim-
ilarity (Xu et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2026), and
DSTP (Kim et al., 2026) triggers when the attention
distribution over kept visual tokens departs from a
prefill anchor. All use fixed thresholds. Our trig-
ger signal is the attention mass on evicted tokens,
which directly bounds the eviction error (Feng et al.,
2025; Shi and Gu, 2026) and is the online counter-

3
The Limits of One-Shot Selection
Setup.
We use Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (Wang
et al., 2024), LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B (Liu et al.,
2024b) and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (Bai et al.,
2025) with FlashAttention-2. The budget is 64
KV entries per KV head: c = 32 selected prefix to-
kens plus the last W = 32 observation-window
tokens; generated tokens are appended.
Tasks
are TextCaps (Sidorov et al., 2020) (500 valida-
tion images, one-sentence captions, ≤64 new to-
kens) and DetailCaps (Dong et al., 2024) (300
images, median 157–238 words, ≤512 new to-
kens). All runs use greedy decoding (details in
Appendix M). Our metric is fidelity: ROUGE-L
F1 between the compressed and the uncompressed
output, which isolates the deviation caused by com-
pression; confidence intervals are paired bootstrap
95%. Reference-based CIDEr and ROUGE-L sep-
arate one-shot from dynamic methods but not dy-
namic methods from each other (§6, Appendix H).
Better scoring is not enough.
Table 1 compares
one-shot selectors on DetailCaps. SnapKV, BA-
CON and last-query scoring differ by at most 2.4
points; selecting with the attention of the first 8
answer tokens (decoded with full KV) adds 4–6
points over BACON, and even the prefill oracle,
which scores with the uncompressed answer’s own
attention, stays at .44–.52. Re-selecting at every
decoding step with the current query reaches .48–
.61. On short-answer VQA the picture is the oppo-
site: the prefill oracle loses no accuracy (Qwen2
TextVQA (Singh et al., 2019) .851 vs. .848 un-
compressed; similarly on DocVQA (Mathew et al.,
2021)), so the gap there is one of scoring. In long-
form generation, we attribute the gap that remains
even under oracle scoring to the staleness of the
selection. Attention trajectories confirm this: the
most-attended prefix position stays the same be-
tween adjacent steps only 41% (Qwen2) to 80%
(LLaVA) of the time, and 37–44% of newly at-
tended evidence in LLaVA lies far from all previous
evidence.

Where the gap comes from.
Figure 2 splits the
300 Qwen2-VL DetailCaps samples into terciles
by their mean drift under τ = 0.03. On high-
drift samples the fixed threshold refreshes 35% of
layer-steps and already matches exact per-step re-
selection (.479 vs. .484), ahead of InfiniGen (.442).
On low-drift samples it refreshes only 1% of the
time, yet these samples gain the most from refresh-
ing (BACON .462, exact .763), and InfiniGen leads
by 16 points. Across samples, the gap to InfiniGen
is unrelated to output length (Spearman −0.01) but
clearly related to mean drift (−0.41; Appendix E).
Normalising drift by the total prefix attention does
not help: the two quantities have a sample-level
rank correlation of 0.98. In low-drift samples lit-
tle attention reaches evicted tokens, but that little
matters.
Takeaway.
Within a sample, drift is a good sig-
nal of when attention has moved; across samples its
magnitude does not reflect how valuable a refresh
is. A single threshold therefore cannot allocate
refreshes well, which motivates controlling the re-
fresh budget per sample.

Rate-Controlled Refresh
Cache layout.
Prefill selects Sl,h with BACON.
The full prefix KV (Np tokens) is kept aside (in
CPU memory in a deployment), and the GPU cache
is laid out as [c selected | W window | generated].
A refresh overwrites only the first c slots; keys carry
RoPE already, so no re-encoding is needed.
Per-sample rate control.
RCR (Algorithm 1 in
Figure 3) maintains one threshold τ per sample,
shared by all layers and initialised to τ0 = 0.03.

see a threshold adjusted by earlier layers’ triggers;
we did not explore per-layer thresholds. We use
ρ = 0.4 throughout.
Low-rank drift detector.
At the end of prefill
we take, per layer and KV head, the top R = 8
right singular vectors PR ∈R128×R of the prefix
key matrix K ∈RNp×128 and keep KPR on the
GPU. Logits of evicted tokens are approximated by
(qPR)(kPR)⊤while kept tokens use exact logits.
This costs the same as the page-mean estimator
(≈Np/16 dot products per step) but resolves in-
dividual tokens and yields a token-level ranking.
The skewing idea follows InfiniGen, which uses 32
channels for per-step selection; RCR uses 8 chan-
nels and only for detection and coarse ranking.
Candidate re-selection.
When a layer triggers,
RCR fetches the keys of the detector’s top 4c to-
kens, computes their exact attention, keeps the top
c, and fetches their values. A refresh thus reads 5c
vectors instead of Np + 2c for a full-prefix scan.
GPU-side decision.
A naive implementation
copies the drift-vs-threshold comparison to the host
in every layer and step to branch on it, which
stalls the kernel pipeline.

Cost.
Per KV head and step, RCR performs ≈
Np/16 detector dot products and reads on average
5c r = 160 r vectors from the CPU, where r ≈ρ
is the refresh rate. Quest, ArkVale and InfiniGen
read 2c = 64 vectors per step, two-stage Quest
5c = 160, and the fixed-threshold rule r(Np + 2c).
The GPU-resident detector of RCR holds Np/16
key equivalents, versus Np/4 for InfiniGen’s 32
channels and for Quest’s page extrema at page size
8.

Experiments
Baselines.
All dynamic methods start from the
same BACON selection and budget, and re-select
with the current step’s attention only. Exact ev-
ery step re-selects from exact full-prefix attention
at each step; it is the strongest current-query re-
selection, not a strict bound (RCR slightly exceeds
it on LLaVA DetailCaps). Periodic re-selects at
step 1 and every T steps. Quest, ArkVale and two-
stage Quest (page size 8; the latter scores 4c can-
didates exactly) retrieve at every step, as does In-
finiGen (previous-layer input as query proxy, 32
of 128 skewed channels). Fixed threshold is the
page-mean drift rule of §4 with τ chosen per setting
by the rule we registered for Qwen3-VL (the most
faithful threshold with a refresh rate of 10–30%;
Appendix C). It also differs from RCR in its detec-
tor and full-prefix refresh (separated in Table 3).
Protocol.
RCR’s components were selected on
a development split of 100 Qwen2-VL DetailCaps
images that overlaps the 300-image test set in only
two images (Appendix A). Three candidates were
then evaluated on all six model–task settings under
a decision rule registered in advance; we report
RCR here and all candidates in Appendix B.
Main results.
Table 2 reports fidelity on all six
settings. Apart from exact per-step re-selection,
RCR is the best method in every setting. It im-
proves over BACON by 11.4–21.0 points on De-
tailCaps and stays within 1.7 points of exact per-
step re-selection in every setting (and above it on
LLaVA DetailCaps), while refreshing 40–48% of
layer-steps. It outperforms InfiniGen with con-
fidence intervals above zero on five settings (+1.6
to +3.8), and is on par with it on Qwen3-VL De-
tailCaps (+0.5, interval including zero). One ρ fits
all settings. The fixed-threshold rule needs a tuned
τ per setting, and on Qwen3-VL its pre-registered
setting falls well behind the per-step methods (Ap-
pendix C); RCR uses the same configuration ev-
erywhere and improves over it by 2.0–4.8 points
on DetailCaps. On TextCaps the realised refresh
rate (41–48%) slightly exceeds ρ because outputs
last only 12–29 steps, too few for the threshold to
converge. Page-level retrieval is ineffective at this
budget: with only four pages per step, Quest gains
at most 6.0 points over BACON and ArkVale falls
below BACON on Qwen2-VL.
Cost.
Figure 4 places each method by its CPU-to-
GPU traffic. RCR reads 63–77 vectors per step and
head (160 r), on par with InfiniGen and Quest (64),
39–48% of two-stage Quest (160), and at most half
of the fixed-threshold rule, which scans the full
prefix at every trigger. Its GPU-resident detector
is 4× smaller than InfiniGen’s, and it changes the
cache on only ∼40% of layer-steps. The one-off
extra cost is an SVD of an Np × 128 matrix per
layer and head at the end of prefill. Our implemen-
tation keeps the full prefix on the GPU to simulate
offloading, so we report traffic rather than wall-
clock latency; moving the trigger decision onto the
GPU alone speeds up our simulation by about 3.4×
(Appendix F).
