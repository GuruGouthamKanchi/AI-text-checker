ABSTRACT
Feed-forward models accurately estimate camera motion and 3D geometry from
short video windows. However, computational and memory costs prevent direct
inference on sequences containing thousands of frames. Long videos must there-
fore be processed as overlapping chunks. This division produces independent
local reconstructions with inconsistent coordinate frames and duplicate estimates
of shared cameras. Achieving global consistency requires propagating constraints
from adjacent overlaps and distant revisits across the sequence. This requirement
naturally motivates pose graph optimization. However, the choice of graph level
creates a trade-off. Chunk-level graphs efficiently resolve scale drift and close
large loops. Yet, their rigid transformations cannot correct internal errors or con-
flicting frame predictions. Conversely, frame-level optimization offers local flex-
ibility, but scaling it globally is computationally expensive and can break metric
scale. HIP3R resolves this trade-off using a hierarchical pose graph. HIP3R
first optimizes a robust chunk-level Sim(3) graph to establish the global layout
and scale. It then holds this layout fixed and estimates one shared SE(3) camera
per frame, turning overlapping window predictions into a single trajectory. Ex-
periments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of HIP3R in
improving camera trajectories and 3D reconstruction. Our method applies to both
chunk-based and streaming backends without additional training or fine-tuning.

INTRODUCTION
Recovering globally consistent 3D geometry and camera trajectories from video is fundamental to
computer vision. This capability supports autonomous navigation, robotic mapping, and spatial
artificial intelligence. Recent 3D foundation models (Wang et al., 2024; 2025a; 2026b) have sig-
nificantly advanced visual geometry. By jointly predicting camera poses and dense pointmaps in
a single forward pass, they bypass classical handcrafted matching. These models achieve high ac-
curacy on short video clips without per-scene calibration or optimization. However, the quadratic
complexity of visual attention limits them to short temporal windows. Directly scaling transformers
to extended multi-kilometer sequences remains too expensive.
To process long videos, modern systems adopt a divide-and-conquer strategy (Deng et al., 2025).
Long sequences are divided into overlapping video chunks, which are processed independently and
stitched together. While chunking bounds GPU memory, sequential alignment introduces severe
computational and numerical bottlenecks. Dense overlapping points share high spatial redundancy,
making all-pixel alignment computationally expensive.
Furthermore, pairwise alignment errors
compound along the sequential chain, causing severe scale and orientation drift. Loop closures
are essential to eliminate cumulative drift and enforce global topological consistency. However,
when the chains are not yet aligned, applying multiple long-range loop constraints at once can make
the non-convex optimization unstable. The solver may diverge or converge to poor local minima.
As shown in Figure 1, even after global chunk placement is resolved, a conflict remains at the frame
level. Existing alignment methods (Deng et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026b; Xiong et al., 2026)
optimize similarity transformations per chunk. However, a single similarity transform preserves

To overcome these challenges, we introduce HIP3R, a Hierarchical Pose-graph optimization method
for long-horizon 3D Reconstruction. HIP3R decomposes reconstruction into coarse chunk layout
recovery and fine frame trajectory refinement. The coarse stage optimizes a chunk-level Sim(3)
pose graph over adjacent overlaps and distant loop closures. This stage establishes a globally con-
sistent layout and eliminates accumulated scale drift. Holding this coarse layout fixed, the fine stage
optimizes a frame-level shared-camera SE(3) pose graph, representing each physical frame with a
single camera variable to resolve cross-window pose inconsistency. Placement priors keep that cam-
era on the recovered layout, while within-chunk motion factors resolve conflicting predictions from
overlapping windows. We evaluate HIP3R across seven public benchmarks spanning diverse indoor
and outdoor environments. Under matched backends and windows, HIP3R improves long-horizon
trajectory accuracy over loop-enabled VGGT-Long. Controlled ablations show that bounded point
sampling reduces graph construction and optimization time with little change in accuracy. Addi-
tional evaluations show that the method also works with streaming backends such as LingBot-MAP.
We summarize our main contributions as follows:
• We show that chunk-level similarity alignment alone cannot ensure cross-window camera-pose
consistency, as overlapping windows may yield conflicting estimates for the same frame.
• We develop a hierarchical pose graph that first recovers a globally consistent chunk layout and
scale, then estimates one shared SE(3) camera pose for each frame.
• We introduce progressive loop closure and bounded point sampling to achieve stable and scalable
optimization on long sequences without retraining.

Streaming and recurrent video geometry. To extend temporal context beyond static windows,
another line of research develops streaming and recurrent architectures. Early methods such as
Spann3R (Wang & Agapito, 2025) and CUT3R (Wang et al., 2025b) maintain persistent spatial
memory states. Recent methods like StreamVGGT (Zhuo et al., 2026), STream3R (Lan et al., 2026),
and WinT3R (Li et al., 2026b) process video frames causally with temporal key-value caching.
LingBot-MAP (Chen et al., 2026a) structures geometric context across anchor frames, local win-
dows, and trajectory memory. Other approaches explore test-time training, memory compression,
and feature retrieval (Chen et al., 2026b; Zhang et al., 2026a; Xie et al., 2026; Jin et al., 2026; Liu

post-processing, HIP3R adds global loops and per-frame pose consistency without retraining.
Multi-window fusion and Structure-from-Motion. Scaling feed-forward predictions to extended
trajectories connects to classical global Structure-from-Motion and pose graph optimization. Chunk-
based methods like VGGT-Long (Deng et al., 2025), VGGT-Align (Zhang et al., 2026b), and VGGT-
Motion (Xiong et al., 2026) align adjacent chunks via pairwise Sim(3) alignment. A rigid Sim(3)
per chunk preserves internal cameras up to scale and cannot resolve overlapping-frame disagree-
ments. GlueMap (Pan et al., 2026) couples feed-forward star reconstructions with global motion
averaging and bundle adjustment over virtual tracks. HIP3R resolves overlapping camera predic-
tions through a shared frame-level pose graph after chunk alignment, without optimizing 3D tracks.

3.2
COARSE LAYOUT OPTIMIZATION WITH PROGRESSIVE LOOP SCHEDULING
We formulate global layout recovery as optimization over a similarity pose graph Gc = (Vc, Ec).
Each node k ∈Vc represents a chunk Ck and is associated with a similarity transformation Ak =
(sk, Rk, tk) ∈Sim(3). This transformation maps local chunk coordinates to a common world
coordinate system as Ak(x) = skRkx + tk. An edge (i, j) ∈Ec represents a measured relative
transformation bZij from the coordinate system of chunk i to that of chunk j. The corresponding
predicted relative transformation is A−1

edges connect retrieved chunk pairs that observe the same physical scene. Relative constraints
determine scale only up to a global factor, so we fix A1 as the world frame and scale reference.
Scale errors in independent chunk predictions accumulate on long trajectories, so we optimize the
remaining sk jointly with rotation and translation.
Dense overlapping 3D points are spatially redundant, so all-pixel alignment is unnecessary. For
adjacent chunks, identical pixels in overlapping frames yield paired 3D predictions. We estimate
relative similarities with confidence-weighted iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) rather than
unweighted Umeyama alignment on all dense pixels (Deng et al., 2025). Each iteration runs a
weighted Umeyama alignment (Umeyama, 1991) and updates Huber weights from spatial residuals.
We bound alignment cost by limiting the number of point correspondences used in each fit.
Visual place recognition proposes candidate loop closures across temporal separations (Izquierdo
& Civera, 2024). For each selected pair of revisiting frames, we combine temporal neighborhoods
from both endpoints into one backend input. The resulting joint reconstruction connects the two
visits in a common local coordinate system. We align the predicted geometry at each endpoint to the
corresponding original chunk, obtaining transformations Bi and Bj from the joint reconstruction to
chunks i and j, respectively. The relative loop measurement is bZij = BjB−1

Activating all distant loops on an unaligned chain can make the non-convex optimization unstable.
We initialize chunk placements from sequential alignments, then introduce loop constraints in order

differentiation. Appendix D provides the parameterization, scheduling rules, and restart settings.

EXPERIMENTS
We evaluate trajectory accuracy across seven public benchmarks comprising 61 diverse sequences.
The benchmark spans indoor room-scale environments, including 7-Scenes (Shotton et al., 2013)
and Neural RGB-D (Azinovi´c et al., 2022). It also includes high-precision multi-scale scenes from
ETH3D (Sch¨ops et al., 2017) and Tanks and Temples (Knapitsch et al., 2017). Finally, it includes ex-
tended outdoor driving and campus traversals from Oxford Spires (Tao et al., 2025), KITTI (Geiger
et al., 2012), and VBR (Brizi et al., 2024). Dense surface geometry is evaluated on ETH3D, Neural
RGB-D, and 7-Scenes, which provide dense 3D ground truth. Appendix E details dataset composi-
tions and sequence splits. Baselines in Table 1 use released checkpoints and recommended window
policies on identical sequences. VGGT-Long and VGGT-Align are evaluated from public imple-
mentations, while VGGT-Motion (Xiong et al., 2026) is not publicly available.
We evaluate trajectory accuracy using ATE, RPErot, and RPEtrans (lower is better). ATE is translation
RMSE after a per-sequence Sim(3) alignment that corrects rotation, translation, and scale. Relative
errors use all consecutive frame pairs (∆=1 frame). Rotation RPE is in degrees, and translation RPE
applies the same Sim(3) correction. Pairwise pose AUC (higher is better) evaluates all i < j camera
pairs using the maximum of relative rotation and translation-direction angular errors at thresholds of

5◦, 15◦, and 30◦. Reconstructed 3D pointmaps are evaluated using the mean of accuracy and com-
pleteness (Chamfer, meters) after Umeyama and ICP. F1 uses a hit distance of 0.25 m on ETH3D and
0.05 m on Neural RGB-D and 7-Scenes. Appendix H provides per-dataset geometric breakdowns.
We apply HIP3R to the camera poses and dense geometry produced by VGGT-Omega (Wang et al.,
2026b) and LingBot-MAP (Chen et al., 2026a). Both backends use the same graph formulation
without fine-tuning. Unless otherwise stated, we use VGGT-Omega 1B with a chunk size of 60
frames and an overlap size of 30 frames. LingBot-MAP uses windowed inference with a chunk size
of 120 frames and an overlap size of 32 frames.
We use the same refinement weights across backends. For short sequences, the fine stage optimizes
all camera poses; for long sequences, it optimizes a keyframe subgraph and interpolates pose cor-
rections for the remaining frames. Appendix details the keyframe selection rules and solver settings.

BENCHMARK EVALUATION ACROSS PUBLIC DATASETS
Table 1 reports tracking metrics macro-averaged across the seven datasets. With VGGT-Omega,
HIP3R reduces ATE by 74%, from 18.555 to 4.906 m, RPErot from 1.820◦to 0.590◦, and RPEtrans

further improves relative pose accuracy while preserving the layout. On compact indoor benchmarks

4.2
CONTROLLED LONG-HORIZON RECONSTRUCTION
We compare HIP3R with two VGGT-Long (Deng et al., 2025) variants, with and without loop
closures. All three use the same VGGT-Omega backend and the default chunk and overlap sizes
specified above. Table 2 reports ATE on KITTI, VBR, and Oxford and the mean over these three
datasets. For reference, it also includes the released VGGT-Long and VGGT-Align (Zhang et al.,

HIP3R achieves lower ATE than both VGGT-Long variants using VGGT-Omega on all three
datasets. Loop closures reduce VGGT-Long ATE from 110.3 to 10.19 m on KITTI and from 85.50
to 41.27 m on VBR. HIP3R further reduces these errors to 9.730 and 22.44 m, nearly halving
the remaining VBR error. VGGT-Long applies loops as rigid Sim(3) transforms to entire chunks.
HIP3R instead jointly optimizes a sparse global layout graph with progressive loop scheduling.
Appendix K shows that the complete solver further improves accuracy and convergence efficiency,
while Appendix F reports stage-wise runtimes.

END-TO-END POSE ACCURACY AND 3D GEOMETRY

Interaction of placement priors and motion constraints. Table 5 compares four ways of forming
a camera trajectory, Single, Unary, Motion, and Shared, from identical predictions and optimized
coarse layouts, with Layout Only as a reference. Layout Only takes the first available prediction for
each frame in chunk order. Single selects the prediction farthest from its chunk boundaries. Neither
performs frame-level optimization. Unary uses overlapping pose observations in a robust unary-only
objective without relative-motion factors. Motion uses within-chunk motion factors without place-
ment priors. Shared combines both factors as in Eq. (6). On both backends, Shared reduces relative
errors over Single and Unary, with a small ATE gain. Motion yields slightly lower relative errors,
but without placement priors, ATE increases to 11.678 and 6.501 m. Placement priors preserve the
recovered layout, while motion factors improve local smoothness.
