HERD: Harness-Evolving Recursive Distillation for Continual Learning
Xun Sun1
Thanh Thi Nguyen2
Thuy-Trang Vu1
Abstract
Continual learning requires a model to keep
acquiring new skills and knowledge without
compromising its existing capabilities. Para-
metric methods suffer from catastrophic for-
getting and loss of plasticity. Non-parametric
methods, such as prompts, skills, and harnesses,
leverage in-context learning but are limited by
context length and lossy plain-text compres-
sion. To effectively combine their complemen-
tary strengths, we propose HERD (Harness-
Evolving Recursive Distillation), a hybrid con-
tinual learning framework that learns efficiently
in the harness and internalizes experience into
cumulative capabilities via distillation.
On
the harness side, HERD continually refines its
skills to capture reusable patterns, while retain-
ing high-residual examples that the skills fail
to capture in a bounded memory. On the pa-
rameter side, HERD performs drift-constrained
distillation with dual anchors, substantially re-
ducing forgetting over long recursive runs. On
the 25-stage task stream of TRACE+17, under
fixed memory and compute budgets, HERD
matches the strongest parametric baseline in
new-task acquisition while exhibiting the least
forgetting and the strongest retention of general
capabilities.

Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) are highly capable,
yet cannot independently carry out much of the
work people need done (Mazeika et al., 2025). One
pressing problem in closing this gap is continual
learning: humans keep learning from experience
while retaining what they know, whereas models
no longer change once deployed. Continual learn-
ing (CL) seeks to enable this ability by learning
from a stream of tasks without forgetting existing
capabilities or retraining from scratch on earlier
data (Wu et al., 2024).

Parametric methods, however, inevitably en-
counter catastrophic forgetting (McCloskey and
Cohen, 1989; Kirkpatrick et al., 2017; Huang et al.,
2024) and loss of plasticity (Dohare et al., 2024;
Yang et al., 2026). They also give little control over
what is internalized: raw experience carries noise,
duplicates, and conflicts alongside the few samples
that matter.
In-context
learning
(ICL)
offers
a
non-
parametric path to continual learning (Brown et al.,
2020; Gutiérrez et al., 2025). New knowledge
is stored in external memory and retrieved into
context, so a frozen LLM learns across tasks (Shi
et al., 2025). This appears to sidestep the flaws
above: a frozen model forgets nothing, loses no
plasticity, and keeps knowledge inspectable and
editable. It is also the norm in practice: users write
prompts, skills, and harnesses rather than post-
train, and recent works build continual learning
systems on harnesses (Zhang et al., 2026c; Kang
et al., 2026). Could a harness alone, recursively
compressing its context, solve continual learning
once models are strong and context windows long
enough? We argue that it cannot. External stores
can hold unbounded data, but nothing accumulates
in the model itself: whatever it acts on must pass
through the context, so experience is compressed
into bounded plain text, which loses information at
a very high rate. The more experience accumulates,
the more is lost.
The resolution lies in the weights themselves.
A few hundred gigabytes of parameters recall his-
tory, law, medicine, and science (Allen-Zhu and
Li, 2025; Morris et al., 2026; Roberts et al., 2020),
or even reproduce entire books nearly verbatim
(Cooper et al., 2026). Language models are general-
purpose compressors (Delétang et al., 2024), and
compression is intelligence (Hutter, 2005). Contin-
ual learning therefore still relies on weight updates,
but should not apply them to every example. Each
layer should store what it is best suited for: capacity
in the weights, selection in the harness.
We therefore propose HERD (Harness-Evolving
Recursive Distillation), a hybrid continual learning

system in which the harness is the fast, selective
learning layer and the weights are the slow, persis-
tent layer. The design mirrors the human brain: a
harness resembles episodic memory formed during
the day, flexible but limited in capacity, which sleep
consolidates into long-term memory (McClelland
et al., 1995). HERD likewise internalizes its har-
ness into the weights after every stage, under fixed
memory and compute budgets and at no added in-
ference cost. It does not eliminate forgetting, but
preserves existing capabilities as far as possible
while the model keeps learning.
HERD is designed around recursion: it must
run stably stage after stage, which raises two chal-
lenges. First, the context is bounded, so the har-
ness must decide what to change and what to keep.
Second, recursive training drifts, and unchecked
drift ends in collapse. HERD answers each with
one module (Figure 1). For the first, the harness
evolves with the task stream: the model iterates
on its skills to abstract reusable rules, and retains
in a bounded memory the high-residual samples
the skills cannot cover. For the second, HERD
performs drift-constrained distillation with dual
anchors. The student is the previous checkpoint
trained to match itself under the harness, which
shifts the model less than SFT and thus forgets less
(Shenfeld et al., 2026, 2025). The previous check-
point bounds per-stage drift and the base model
bounds cumulative drift. The harness is kept, in-
spectable and editable, for the next stage; only the
weights are deployed.
Across a 25-stage stream HERD keeps more of
what it learns than any other method: its final mean
over all seen tasks is 0.706, above every parametric
baseline and 27 points above the non-parametric
ones, while its acquisition, 0.713, is statistically in-
distinguishable from the plasticity floor set by Seq-
LoRA (Table 1). Forgetting is 0.010, about half
or less that of every weight-updating baseline but
SDFT, whose generation collapses (Appendix D);
the general test falls 6.3 points where these other
baselines lose 20.0 to 25.5. In Figure 1, the rule
learned at stage 5 still holds in the weights alone at
stage 12 across three seeds.
Our contributions are: (1) HERD, a hybrid con-
tinual learning system that evolves a harness of
skills and high-residual memory and recursively
internalizes it into the weights (§2); (2) drift-
constrained distillation with dual anchors, where
the previous checkpoint bounds per-stage drift and
the base model bounds cumulative drift (§2.4); (3)
an evaluation on TRACE+17, a 25-stage stream
of eight TRACE tasks (Wang et al., 2023b) and
17 Super-NaturalInstructions tasks (Wang et al.,
2022), against parametric, non-parametric, and hy-
brid baselines (§3).

Harness representation.
We use harness in the
sense of DeepSeek Harness (DSH) (DeepSeek-
AI, 2026) and of harness-level continual learning
(Kang et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a): the state
and procedures around a model at inference, such
as system prompts, skills, memories, and tools, as
opposed to its weights. The harness of HERD is
H = (K, R). K is a registry of skills, each a rout-
ing prefix and instructions; an input x is routed
by prefix, and the matched instructions K(x) be-
come the system message. R is a residual memory
of whole training records, prompt and verified an-
swer, kept verbatim. The budgets are BK and BR,
with BM = BK + BR. Executing the harness
is f(x; θ, K): the model θ answering x under the
system message K(x).
2.2
HERD in One Stage
In this notation, HERD after stage t is Lt =
(f¯θt, (Kt, Rt, G)): merged dense weights ¯θt, the
harness Ht = (Kt, Rt), and a fixed set G of gen-
eral probes reserved before the stream. Figure 1
shows one stage, which learns the task twice. First,
the harness evolves on a bounded window of the
task (§2.3). Second, the checkpoint, given the skill
and the verified answer, teaches a fresh adapter
that sees only the bare prompt, under drift budgets
toward ¯θt−1 and θ0 (§2.4); the adapter is merged
into ¯θt, the memory is refreshed under the merged
weights, and the harness is carried on. Both steps
are driven by the residual of a record (Eq. 2).

3
Experiments
Our experiments evaluate HERD under a unified
protocol to answer three questions. Q1. Does
HERD outperform purely parametric and purely
non-parametric methods? Q2. As the number
of CL stages grows, does HERD maintain long-
horizon stability? Q3. Are harness evolution, high-
residual memory, and the two drift anchors all in-
dispensable?
3.1
Experimental Settings
Benchmark.
TRACE+17 appends 17 Super-
NaturalInstructions tasks (Wang et al., 2022) to the
eight TRACE tasks (Wang et al., 2023b), because
eight stages are too few to tell stability from slow
collapse. Each of the 25 tasks has 500 training,
100 validation, and 100 test examples; no prompt
appears in more than one task or split. A G-test
of five categories, 200 held-out items each, tracks
general capability: knowledge, mathematics, com-
monsense, instruction following, and multilingual
understanding (MMLU, GSM8K, HellaSwag, IFE-
val, XCOPA; Hendrycks et al., 2020; Cobbe et al.,
2021; Zellers et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2023; Ponti
et al., 2020). All methods start from Qwen3.5-4B
with the same decoding, length limits, and scorers;
all weight updates share one budget, 32 updates of
32 records per stage. After every stage we score the
current task; after stages 4, 8, 16, and 25 we also
score all seen tasks and the G-test (Appendix B).
Baselines.
Non-parametric: ACE (Zhang et al.,
2026c) grows a playbook; HCL (Kang et al., 2026)
keeps a harness edit only if it improves the cur-
rent task and breaks no retained example; both
attach the harness to every prompt at inference.
Parametric: Seq-LoRA trains one adapter through
the stream and serves as the floor; uniform re-
play keeps a memory the size of HERD’s residual
memory, filled by uniform sampling; OPR (Chen
et al., 2026) replays the model’s own best earlier an-
swers; TreeLoRA (Qian et al., 2025) and MoRAM
(Lu et al., 2026) isolate updates, in a gradient-
similarity tree and in rank-1 experts; SDFT (Shen-
feld et al., 2026) self-distills from a demonstration-
conditioned teacher, without a harness. Hybrid:
OPHSD (Zhao et al., 2026) distills a fixed harness
into the weights and deploys without it; the harness
never changes and drift is not budgeted. Controls:
the base model as is, and the same model under a
fixed harness that never learns.
Metrics.
Acquisition, the mean of Rj,j, is each
task’s score right after it is learned; AA is what
remains after stage 25; F is what is lost in between
(Eq. 1). We report both the mean change over the
five G-test categories, ∆C, and the worst, ∆Cmin,
because a collapse in one category can hide be-
hind a mean of five (§2.4). All numbers are means
over three seeds, with standard deviations and 95%
bootstrap intervals over items.

Main Results

frontier of the three families: it acquires as much
