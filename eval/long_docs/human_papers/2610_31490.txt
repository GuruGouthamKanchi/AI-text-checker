Self-Supervised Multibeam Sonar Restoration via
Coherent Forward Modeling
Zixin Wang, James Hopgood, and Mike Davies
Abstract—Multibeam imaging sonar produces intensity images
degraded by coherent speckle and system-induced blur, while
clean reflectivity targets are rarely available. Existing despeckling
methods often rely on simplified intensity-domain noise models,
synthetic clean targets, or blind-spot self-supervision, and typi-
cally do not jointly model deblurring and speckle suppression.
We propose a physics-consistent self-supervised framework for
joint despeckling and deblurring of multibeam imaging sonar. A
stochastic coherent image-formation model yields a conditional-
mean forward operator that relates latent scene reflectivity to
expected intensity through the squared magnitude of the system
point spread function. We then use registered multi-frame sonar
sequences to train without clean ground truth images, showing
that, under an idealized inter-frame conditional-mean condition,
the expected paired loss equals the corresponding forward-
prediction loss up to a network-independent constant. To stabilize
the ill-conditioned inverse problem, we further adapt robust
equivariant imaging using stochastic coherent simulation and
introduce a paired forward prediction error metric for ground-
truth-free evaluation. Experiments on two real multibeam sonar
datasets demonstrate a favourable balance between forward-
prediction agreement, homogeneous-region speckle suppression,
and preservation of visible structural boundaries compared with
representative baselines.
Index Terms—Multibeam imaging sonar, coherent image for-
mation, spatially correlated speckle, self-supervised image recon-
struction, equivariant imaging.
I. INTRODUCTION
M
ULTIBEAM imaging sonar is widely used for under-
water perception, mapping, inspection, and robotic nav-
igation because it remains effective in turbid water and low-
visibility environments [1], [2], [3]. However, sonar images are
often degraded by coherent speckle and system-induced blur,
which obscure small targets, weaken object boundaries, and
reduce the reliability of downstream tasks such as detection,
segmentation, and scene interpretation [4], [5], [6]. Effective
restoration therefore requires not only speckle suppression, but
also recovery of structurally faithful and spatially sharp image
content.
A central difficulty is that practical imaging sonars usu-
ally provide detected intensity images, while the dominant
degradations arise from coherent acoustic image formation.
The beamformed complex field is formed by the coherent
superposition of echoes from unresolved scatterers and is
subsequently converted to intensity. This process gives rise to
signal-dependent, non-Gaussian speckle rather than simple ad-
ditive noise [7], [8], [9]. In addition, the system response acts

on the coherent field before intensity detection, so speckle cor-
relation and blur are coupled through the effective point spread
function (PSF) of the imaging system [10], [11], [12]. Since
clean reflectivity ground truth is rarely available, imaging-
sonar restoration must address a coupled inverse problem
without ground truth: joint despeckling and deblurring from
intensity-only observations.
Existing sonar despeckling methods, together with the
broader Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) despeckling litera-
ture, are often based on simplified intensity-domain statisti-
cal models. Classical approaches commonly treat speckle as
multiplicative noise, frequently with exponential or Gamma
statistics, and sometimes transform it into an additive form
in the logarithmic domain [10], [13], [14], [15]. These as-
sumptions have motivated local adaptive filters, Bayesian
estimators, diffusion-based methods, and nonlocal patch ag-
gregation approaches [16], [17], [18], [19]. However, these
formulations primarily describe marginal or locally filtered
intensity statistics. Spatial correlation is typically introduced
through filtering windows, patch matching, aggregation, or
hand-specified correlation structures [20]. Consequently, they
do not provide a sonar-specific analytical observation model
in which spatially correlated speckle and point spread function
(PSF) blur are generated jointly.
Learning-based despeckling methods can achieve strong
empirical performance, but supervised training generally re-
quires clean reflectivity targets, which are rarely obtainable
in real sonar imaging [15], [21], [22], [23], [24]. Many
methods therefore rely on synthetic clean images, simulated
speckle corruption, or architectures transferred from optical
denoising [25], [26]. These strategies alleviate the lack of
labeled sonar data, but they can introduce a mismatch between
the training degradation and real sonar observations, where
coherent speckle, spatial correlation, system blur, platform mo-
tion, and sensor-specific effects are coupled by the acquisition
process. A recent despeckling method adopts a multiplicative
y = xn model with Gamma-distributed speckle and constrains
log-domain residual statistics [27]. However, its formulation
remains a single-image despeckling framework and does not
explicitly model PSF-coupled coherent image formation or the
spatially correlated speckle induced by the imaging process.
Self-supervised methods reduce the dependence on clean
targets by constructing supervision from noisy measurements
themselves. Blind-spot, masking-based, and subsampling-
based approaches prevent the network from directly observing
the prediction target and have been widely used for image
despeckling [28], [29], [30], [31], [32], [33], [34], [35], [36].
These methods usually rely on a conditional-independence

assumption: the corruption at the prediction target should be
independent of the visible measurements given the underlying
clean signal. This assumption is difficult to satisfy in imaging
sonar because coherent propagation and the system response
introduce spatially correlated speckle. Enlarging the blind spot
or excluding neighbouring pixels can reduce noise leakage,
but it also removes local spatial context that is important for
preserving structure and undoing system blur.
Noisy-pair learning provides a complementary route to self-
supervision. Noise2Noise showed that clean targets can be
replaced by independently corrupted observations with the
same conditional mean, and Frame2Frame extended this idea
to registered neighbouring video frames [37], [38]. Related
principles have also been explored for multitemporal SAR
despeckling and registered sonar sequences [39], [40]. In
coherent sonar, however, neighbouring frames are not simply
independent additive-noise observations: they are affected by
viewpoint change, platform motion, registration error, coherent
speckle decorrelation, and the system point spread function.
Therefore, a registered-frame objective must specify not only
how frames are paired, but also which physical domain the
network output represents.
Our preliminary work, PI-SSD [40], used registered sonar
frame pairs for self-supervised despeckling. However, it
matches the network output directly to the second observation,

2, without applying the PSF-dependent forward
operator to the output. The network therefore targets a still
PSF-blurred intensity image.
Building on PI-SSD, the present work uses the coherent
image-formation model explicitly in the learning objective
to formulate a self-supervised inverse problem, rather than
treating paired frames as generic noisy images. We derive
the conditional mean intensity learning goal for the coher-
ent speckle-contaminated forward model, showing how the
expected intensity observation is related to the scene reflec-
tivity and the squared magnitude of the system PSF. This
provides a physically meaningful forward prediction target
for training from registered multi-frame sonar observations
without clean reflectivity ground truth. The resulting inverse
problem remains ill-conditioned, since different reflectivity
estimates can produce similar intensity observations. We there-
fore extend robust equivariant imaging (REI) [41], [42] to
sonar restoration by adapting it to the derived conditional
mean learning goal and incorporating it within a Noise2Noise-
type self-supervised training framework. This combines a
physics-consistent paired loss, which exploits registered sonar
frames with approximately decorrelated speckle, with REI
regularization to constrain the reconstruction under known
transformations. Finally, using the same paired-frame proto-
col, we introduce paired forward prediction error (PFPE) to
assess ground-truth-free forward-prediction consistency with
registered paired observations.
This work treats multibeam-sonar restoration as a physics-
consistent self-supervised inverse problem, rather than as
generic image denoising. The network estimates a latent
reflectivity image, and supervision is imposed only after pro-
jection through a coherent conditional-mean forward model.
This distinguishes the proposed framework from blind-spot
denoising and direct noisy-pair despeckling methods, which
operate primarily in the observation/image domain. The main
contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
• We develop a coherent-formation-based statistical obser-
vation model for imaging sonar restoration. The model
explains spatially correlated speckle and system-induced
blur as coupled consequences of coherent propagation
and the sonar PSF, rather than imposing speckle correla-
tion through intensity-domain post-filtering.
• We derive the corresponding conditional mean intensity
learning goal for the coherent speckle-contaminated for-
ward model. This provides a physics-consistent forward
prediction objective for learning from detected intensity
measurements without requiring clean reflectivity ground
truth.
• We propose a self-supervised reconstruction framework
for joint despeckling and deblurring using registered
multi-frame sonar observations. Unlike direct noisy-
pair matching, the paired loss constrains the forward
projection Afθ(y1) of the latent reconstruction, while
REI regularization uses stochastic coherent simulation
from the same image-formation model to reduce the ill-
conditioning of sonar intensity reconstruction.
• Finally, based on the same multi-frame analysis, we intro-
duce paired forward prediction error (PFPE) as a ground-
truth-free measure of measurement-domain forward-
prediction consistency under a fixed paired-frame proto-
col. PFPE provides a complementary evaluation criterion
for real sonar data where clean reflectivity ground truth
is unavailable.

IV. EXPERIMENTS
We evaluate the proposed framework on two real multibeam
imaging sonar datasets acquired with a Tritech Gemini 720ik
sonar. In the absence of clean reflectivity ground truth, real-
sonar evaluation cannot directly measure latent reflectivity ac-
curacy. However, when registered pairs of corrupted sonar ob-
servations are available, the same Noise2Noise principle used
for training provides a paired-frame proxy for measurement-
domain predictive consistency. We therefore assess restora-
tion using complementary evidence: the proposed paired for-
ward prediction error (PFPE) measures measurement-domain
forward-prediction consistency, while equivalent number of
looks (ENL), edge preservation index (EPI), and visual com-
parisons assess speckle suppression, edge preservation, and
structural plausibility. In this setting, deblurring is evaluated
indirectly through consistency with the sonar forward model
and preservation of visible structures.

B. Datasets
We use two datasets acquired with a Tritech Gemini 720ik
sonar. The sensor outputs intensity images formed from 512
beams, operates at a centre frequency of 720 kHz, and provides
a 120◦field of view. Raw measurements are represented as
range-beam images in polar coordinates and treated as single-
channel intensity images.
Boat. The Boat dataset contains complex underwater scenes
with cluttered object responses and strong background speckle,
seabed texture, acoustic shadows, and heterogeneous scattering
from object boundaries and surrounding structures. These
factors make the dataset challenging for joint deblurring and
despeckling, since the method must suppress coherent speckle
while preserving structural details and boundaries. It was
acquired with a maximum imaging range of 50 m and a frame
rate of 3 frames per second. The dataset contains 1455 raw
frames.
Underwater Structure. The Underwater Structure dataset
contains more regular structural scenes and provides a com-
plementary evaluation under relatively stable underwater in-
spection conditions. It was acquired with a maximum imaging
range of 25.5 m and a frame rate of 10 frames per second.
The dataset contains 1282 raw frames.
C. Implementation Details
For each raw sequence, we use temporally contiguous train-
ing, validation, and test blocks with an approximate 8 : 1 : 1
ratio before fixed 512 × 512 patch extraction. A small buffer
of unused frames is inserted between adjacent blocks, and
paired-frame samples are formed only within the same split.
This prevents temporally adjacent or registered pairs from
crossing the train/validation/test boundaries. During testing,
the fully convolutional network is applied to individual sonar
frames. We compare the proposed method with representative
model-based despeckling methods, including PPB [18] and
SAR-BM3D [19], and recent learning-based self-supervised
baselines, including Noise2Void [29], Blind2Unblind (B2UB)
[35], LG-BPN [33], SS-BSN [34], SEGSID [36], and PI-SSD
[40].
All paired-frame experiments use a fixed main protocol with
frame gap 1. The gap is chosen as the smallest reliable gap that
reduces inter-frame correlation while preserving scene match
and registration quality. Pairs are retained only when the es-
timated translation satisfies |∆ρ1| ≤10 pixels and |∆ρ2| ≤5
pixels, which removes pairs with large apparent motion, scene
mismatch, or failed registration. This yields 1328 and 1137
retained registered pairs for the Boat and Underwater Structure
datasets, respectively, under the final pairing protocol. The
full gap-correlation statistics and displacement diagnostics
are reported in Supplementary Section S2. All methods are
evaluated using the same fixed test pairs.
The PSF is generated from the Gemini 720ik geometry and
parameters reported in Section IV-B, together with a sound
speed of 1540 m/s and a 96-element receive aperture with
half-wavelength spacing.
For the network, we use a standard U-Net backbone im-
plemented with deepinv [48]. This compact four-scale U-
Net keeps the inference model lightweight and is chosen
to support future low-latency deployment on robotic sonar
platforms. All models are trained with Adam using a learning
rate of 1 × 10−4, a weight decay of 5 × 10−7, and a batch
size of 2. The checkpoint with the lowest validation loss
is selected for testing. Unless otherwise stated, the main
experiments use J = 6, with Tτ implemented as random
rotations with a maximum angle of 20◦for REI regularization.
The REI weight is set to α = 0.03 for Boat and α = 0.05
for Underwater Structure, based on validation performance.
This dataset-specific choice reflects the different registration
accuracy and structural complexity of the two datasets, and no
test-set results were used for this selection. Experiments are
implemented in PyTorch 2.2.2 and run on a single NVIDIA
RTX 3090 GPU.
D. Performance on Boat Dataset
Quantitative evaluation. The Boat results are reported in the
first four metric columns of Table I, and the corresponding
metric distributions are shown in Fig. 3. Additional qualitative
examples and complete metric values are provided in Sup-
plementary Section S3, including Fig. 7 and Table V. PFPE
measures the raw held-out forward-prediction error, while
PFPEagg
imp reports the aggregate improvement over the noisy
registered-pair baseline.
The proposed method achieves the lowest PFPE and the
highest aggregate PFPE improvement of 14.10% under the
stated common latent-reflectivity scoring protocol, indicating
the strongest overall forward-prediction consistency on the
Boat dataset. Blind2Unblind (B2UB) obtains a similar aggre-
gate PFPE improvement of 13.39%, but its ENL decreases
from the raw image value of 8.18 to 7.74, indicating lim-
ited speckle suppression. In contrast, the proposed method
increases ENL to 12.81 while keeping DEPI among the
lowest restored results. Methods with larger ENL, such as SS-
BSN, LG-BPN, and PI-SSD, show much larger edge-strength
deviation and lower PFPE improvement, suggesting that their
higher smoothness is associated with oversmoothing rather
than stronger forward-prediction agreement. The ablation re-
sults further show why PFPE should not be interpreted in
isolation. As reported in Supplementary Section S5-A and
