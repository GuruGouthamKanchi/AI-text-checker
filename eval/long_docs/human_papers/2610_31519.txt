Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) are increas-
ingly used to generate domain-specific com-
mercial & medical content (e.g., claims, state-
ments, and promotional phrases) in regulated
industries such as pharmaceuticals. However,
ensuring its regulatory compliance, factual ac-
curacy, and audience-specific personalization
remains a major challenge. We report a produc-
tion case study asking whether moving compli-
ance and personalization signal from prompt-
level rules into retrieval-time structured con-
text improves pharmaceutical claims genera-
tion, using Composer, a deployed internal tool
built on a Golden Data Model (GDM) of 1,867
pharmaceutical claims spanning four products.
The GDM integrates claim metadata, refer-
ences, audience labels, material type, usage
history, Match Text Variant (MTV) links, and
a six-dimension quality score (6C). Across
six product-audience segments, the context
pipeline improves intended indication (+29%),
intended content type (+62%) and intended tar-
get audience (+67%), 6C quality score (+86%),
and claim reuse (+34%). Production log analy-
sis over the deployment period shows a 29.5%
reduction in weekly API cost. We report this as
descriptive deployment evidence. This work is
a systems and data-architecture contribution: it
shows that in a regulated, high-stakes domain,
restructuring claim data for retrieval-time fil-
tering is a more scalable lever than expanding
prompt-level rules, and documents the engi-
neering path data cleaning, graph linking, and
quality scoring Check our Composer Video.

Introduction
AI-generated text has been detected increasingly in
medical literature (Wolfrath et al., 2026). A 2025
market forecast projected the AI in medical writing
market to reach USD 1,760.02 million by 2030,
with a CAGR of 12.52% from 2025 to 2030 (Grand
View Research, 2025). However, their deployment
in regulated domains such as healthcare and phar-
maceuticals remains constrained by persistent con-
cerns around hallucination, regulatory compliance,
and limited controllability (Hager et al., 2024).
Pharmacy content generation is particularly chal-
lenging. Claims must be: (i) factually grounded
in approved sources, (ii) compliant with regula-
tory constraints, (iii) tailored to specific audiences
such as healthcare professionals (HCPs) or patients
(Barrias et al., 2024).
Our baseline relies on rule-based prompting
and strict templates. In our deployment, this base-
line often failed to produce personalized and con-
textually appropriate outputs. As observed in real-
world systems, rule-based approaches can lead
to mismatches in target audience, low relevance,
and overuse of inactive or general claims.
In contrast, context-rich approaches leverage
structured data, usage history, and semantic rela-
tionships to guide generation. However, the ef-
fectiveness of such approaches has not been ex-
perimentally studied. In this paper, we address
following core research question:
Does shifting compliance and personalization
signals from prompt-level rules to structured
retrieval-time data improve the quality and pro-
duction efficiency of pharmaceutical claim gen-
eration in a deployed system?
To answer this, we propose a golden data
model schema that transforms fragmented claim
data into a structured, graph-based representation
with quality-aware filtering. We further evaluate
rule-based and context-based generation pipelines
across multiple dimensions, including compliance
(e.g., prohibited words / phrases, target indica-
tion / type, MTV-Variant claims reuse), person-
alization (e.g. target audience), and quality (6C
scores). We note that the context pipeline does not
eliminate rules outright, claim-level guardrails and
prohibited-word constraints still persist, but relo-
cates them from static, product-level prompt text

Related Work
Pharmaceutical content management:
Veeva
PromoMats supports promotional content and
claims management,
including substantiation
and contextual claim variations (Veeva Systems,
n.d.).
IQVIA provides HCP engagement so-
lutions (IQVIA, n.d.).
These product descrip-
tions do not establish the internal generation ar-
chitectures of the platforms.
Our contribution
is a production evaluation of an explicit claim-
centered schema and audience-conditioned genera-
tion pipeline. Prior research has examined the in-
formational and emotional content of prescription-
drug advertising (Frosch et al., 2007) and the
distinction between promotion and scientific ex-
change (Kessler, 1991).
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) in
biomedical NLP: RAG (Gao et al., 2023; Asai
et al., 2024) improves grounding by incorporat-
ing external knowledge sources.
RAG has im-
proved factuality in knowledge-intensive genera-
tion (Lewis et al., 2020), whereas chain-of-thought
prompting has improved arithmetic, commonsense,
and symbolic reasoning (Wei et al., 2022); the
latter does not by itself establish reduced hallu-
cination. However, standard RAG systems lack
domain-specific constraints such as regulatory com-
pliance and structured claim validation. RAG alone
is insufficient for pharmaceutical claim generation
because it does not enforce the audience-specific
stylistic transformation that distinguishes a KOL-
facing mechanism statement from a patient-facing
aspirational statement derived from the same in-
put. Prior work in biomedical NLP, has mostly
focused on information extraction and summariza-
tion, but less attention has been given to controlled,
personalized generation under strict regulatory re-
quirements. Our work differs in two key aspects:
(1) we introduce a data-centric framework for con-
trolling generation, and (2) we provide an empirical
comparison between rule-based and context-based
approaches in real-world setting.
Verification and hallucination evaluation: Re-
cent benchmarks such as SciVer (Wang et al., 2025)
evaluate multimodal scientific claim verification
with expert-annotated supporting evidence. Hon-
estVQA (Tripathi et al., 2025) studies confidence
calibration in document visual question answering,
while Decomposed NLI (DNLI) (Yanuka et al.,
2025) evaluates individual propositions in image
captions. MLLMGuard (Gu et al., 2024) assesses
multimodal safety across privacy, bias, toxicity,
truthfulness, and legality. Industrial traffic-incident
benchmarks also examine LLM hallucination (Li
et al., 2025). These studies concern distinct tasks
and do not directly validate pharmaceutical claim
generation.

Methodology
Given a target audience (e.g., HCP, Patient), con-
tent type (e.g., Email, Banner), and a set of claims,
the goal is to generate compliant and personalized
text. The rule-based approach relies on predefined
instructions, including: strict citation rules, verba-
tim claim usage constraints, and template-based
generation. While this ensures high compliance, it
limits flexibility and personalization. We propose
a golden data model schema that enhances claim
usability via 4 key components as shown in Fig 2:
1. Semantic Integrity Layer: periodic pipeline
executing high-volume "upserts" from Veeva Vault
and other sources into the central library. Claims
are clustered using keywords and embeddings to
identify duplicates, near-duplicates, contradictions.
2. Graph-Based Representation: Claims are
linked to: references, materials, audience metadata,
forming a relational graph structure (as shown in
Appendix Figure 6. This is the relationship build-
ing layer. Establishes the structural links between
Claims, References, and Materials. It aggregates
metadata to provide a unified view of derivation
and utilization, stored in Graph DB.
3. 6C Quality Framework: Each claim is then
scored along six dimensions: Clarity, Complete-
ness, Consistency, Connectivity, Compliance, Con-
text, as shown in Table 10 how weights differ, Table
11 why each dimension is scored this way, Table 12
what it evaluates. It translates relational evidence
into a mathematical "Strength Score", quantifies
how firmly a claim to its supporting scientific text.
4. Alignment Annotation Protocol. Indication,
content-type, and target audience alignment were
then judged by internal US medical-content writers
team not involved in system development, using
binary match against the campaign brief’s stated
indication/content-type/ audience fields. Judgment
was BLINDED to pipeline (Pre vs. Post).
5.
Audience Lens: Claims are filtered and
ranked based on target audience, region, and us-
age patterns. This is the reconciliation action layer
- the final decision gate. Based on 6C scores, sta-
tus, and recency, it executes the "Golden Actions"
(Merge/Suppress/Flag), publishing the final, high-
integrity records to the Gold Layer Graph DB.
In our context-based approach, the LLM then
retrieves top-ranked claims, applies semantic filter-
ing and ranking, generates outputs conditioned on
high-quality, audience-specific claims.

Dataset and Experimental Settings
We construct our dataset of more than 1,800 phar-
maceutical claims across 4 products: Hemlibra,
Ocrevus, Ocrevus Zunovo, and Vabysmo. The
claims span 11 categories, including disease ed-
ucation, efficacy, safety, dosing and administration,
clinical practice guidelines, and patient access. All
claims are retrieved from Veeva and are associated
with metadata such as references, usage history,
and audience annotations. We evaluate the two
pipelines using Claude Sonnet 4.5, the production
model for Composer; we additionally report per-
token cost benchmarks for Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash,
the deployment comparison in §5 uses Sonnet 4.5
throughout (Google Cloud, n.d.; Anthropic, 2025b)

Conclusion
This work demonstrates that structured context
moving compliance and personalization signal
from prompt-level rules into retrieval-time claim
metadata provides a more scalable and effective
architecture for content generation in a regulated
industry than expanding prompt-level rules alone.
We evaluated across compliance, target-audience,
indication, content-type alignment, MTV reuse,
latency, token usage, request success rate, and
API cost. We also formalized 6C Algorithm for
evaluating claim quality across Clarity, Complete-
ness, Consistency, Connectivity, Compliance, and
Context Availability. Our results show that the
context-based pipeline improves compliance while
reducing total weekly cost. Although richer re-
trieval increases the average cost per request, the
overall system becomes more efficient - cleaner out-
puts, and a higher success rate. This work demon-
