Abstract
Controllable 3D part generation is pivotal for digital asset creation, yet existing
methods lack the flexibility to accommodate diverse user workflows requiring
varying levels of control. In this paper, we introduce FlexPart, a unified image-
to-3D part generation framework that supports variable-granularity geometric
conditions within a single model. By seamlessly integrating heterogeneous inputs,
ranging from sparse points to dense bounding boxes and masks, via gated adaptive
modulation, FlexPart unlocks a powerful cross-modal synergy. We also propose an
asymmetric geometric guidance mechanism that leverages strong spatial priors to
align representations across granularities, improving geometric consistency and
facilitating inter-task synergy. Under mask conditioning on PartVerseXL, FlexPart
reduces Part CD by 16.6% relative to TripoSG + SAM3D and by 10.5% relative to
OmniPart, while maintaining competitive whole-mesh quality. The code can be
found at https://github.com/Gucci233/FlexPart

Introduction
Perceiving objects as compositions of semantically coherent parts is essential for various tasks such
as animation, simulation, and interactive editing. Unlike holistic 3D generation methods that generate
whole entities [16, 39, 40, 48], image-to-3D part generation decomposes visual inputs into distinct,
spatially assemblable, structurally detachable, and image-consistent 3D components.
However, this task presents significant challenges due to the inherent ambiguity of part decomposition.
For example, the same object can be segmented at various granularities, and users may require
different structural specifications depending on their goals. A straightforward solution is to project
2D segmentation masks [45] onto generated 3D meshes. However, such decoupled pipelines can
introduce geometric inconsistencies during the lifting process. To address this limitation, recent
approaches have integrated part control directly. Some methods rely on dense geometric prompts,
such as pixel-wise 2D segmentation masks [3, 9, 46] or explicit bounding box layouts [8]. These
inputs provide strong spatial constraints, enabling well-structured part generation. In contrast, other
methods explore lighter forms of supervision, such as high-level prompts like part counts [20] or
implicit volumetric partitioning [35]. These approaches reduce annotation effort and improve usability
but often sacrifice precise spatial control.
While effective within their respective domains, existing methods are typically tailored to a single
prompt granularity, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a). This specialization not only limits user flexibility by
hindering seamless transitions between fine-grained control via dense masks and lightweight interac-
tion via sparse points, but also overlooks the inherent structural relationships across different prompt
modalities. Such rigidity is suboptimal, as prompts of varying granularities provide complementary
information that could be jointly leveraged. This raises a fundamental question: can we develop a
unified framework that accommodates variable-granularity inputs for flexible part generation, while
exploiting their cross-modal synergy to enhance quality?
Driven by this motivation, we propose FlexPart, a unified framework for controllable 3D part
generation. As shown in Fig. 1(a), FlexPart integrates sparse points, bounding boxes, and masks
into a single Diffusion Transformer (DiT) via gated adaptive modulation. Joint training improves
generation quality across prompt granularities. To further amplify this synergy, we introduce an
Asymmetric Geometric Guidance mechanism. During training, it employs differentiable optimal
transport to let strong spatial priors (masks/boxes) explicitly supervise the ambiguous representations
of sparse points. The resulting model supports different prompt types at inference without retraining.
By consolidating diverse geometric constraints, FlexPart establishes a unified paradigm for versatile
part creation with variable granularity. Under mask conditioning on PartVerseXL, FlexPart reduces
Part CD by 16.6% relative to TripoSG + SAM3D and by 10.5% relative to OmniPart. Thanks to the
learned cross-modal synergy, it remains highly competitive even with significantly sparser prompts.
Ultimately, FlexPart delivers superior part-level generation quality (Fig. 1(b)) while preserving strong
whole-mesh reconstruction fidelity.
Our contributions are summarized as follows: 1) We propose FlexPart, the first unified framework
integrating point, box, and mask prompts into a single DiT to unlock cross-modal synergy for
controllable 3D part generation; 2) We devise an Asymmetric Geometric Guidance mechanism that
effectively transfers structural knowledge from dense priors to enhance the spatial reasoning of
sparse inputs; 3) We demonstrate that our method achieves superior generation quality and geometric
consistency compared to existing state-of-the-art approaches across diverse input modalities.

Related Work
General 3D Mesh Generation.
Early 3D generation methods relied on optimization-based
pipelines, using Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) to distill 2D priors into implicit 3D represen-
tations [19, 28, 37, 38]. While pioneering, these approaches often suffer from slow convergence
and geometric artifacts. Feed-forward Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) [11, 34] improve
efficiency by directly predicting 3D representations from images. Native 3D generative models
use compact latent representations [5, 47, 48] to support efficient diffusion training. Recent meth-
ods [16, 40, 42, 49] further improve geometric fidelity through structured sparse latents or flow
matching. These object-level generators typically produce holistic meshes without explicit part de-
composition, limiting part-level interaction and editing. Complementary work connects 3D analysis
2


and generation. ROAD transfers discriminative semantic and structural priors to a generative model
through reciprocal-objective alignment [24], while HERMES and HERMES++ jointly model scene
understanding and future geometry generation in autonomous driving [51, 52]. Our work focuses on
joint learning across geometric prompt granularities for controllable object-part generation.
3D Shape Analysis and Part Segmentation. Point-cloud analysis has advanced through efficient
state-space backbones such as PointMamba [18] and model adaptation methods, including dynamic
adapters with prompt tuning [53], spectral-domain fine-tuning [17], and input-aware parameter
adaptation for 3D scenes [23]. Here, learned prompts used for model adaptation differ from the
user-provided geometric prompts considered in our work. Language-driven approaches such as
ShapeLLM [30] and Kestrel [2] further support object understanding and part-aware grounding. For
part decomposition, supervised methods [26, 29] analyze existing shapes, while other approaches
lift 2D foundation model priors, such as SAM [14] and GLIP [15], to 3D [1, 13, 21, 36, 45, 50].
Recent methods learn open-world 3D feature fields or native segmentation networks [22, 25, 44].
Conventional segmentation primarily identifies regions of existing surfaces. HoloPart [43] extends
this setting by completing initially segmented regions into full 3D parts using a generative model.
These pipelines rely on input geometry, whereas our task is to generate complete parts directly from
an image and geometric prompts.
Part-level 3D Generation. Recent generative models synthesize objects as compositions of se-
mantic components, with different ways of specifying part structure. Mask- and layout-conditioned
methods [8, 9, 46] provide explicit spatial control, although precise masks or layouts can require
substantial annotation effort. AutoPartGen [4] generates parts autoregressively and supports con-
ditioning on images, part masks, or existing 3D objects. In a related programmatic reconstruction
setting, MeshCoder [6] uses an LLM to translate point clouds into editable, part-structured mesh
code. Methods such as PartCrafter [20] and PartPacker [35] internalize part decomposition within
the generative process without requiring explicit geometric prompts. This simplifies interaction but
offers less direct control over spatial part boundaries. A related challenge arises in count-controlled
video generation, where NUMINA [33] uses countable latent layouts to improve numerical alignment,
illustrating the value of spatial structure for abstract count-based control. Our framework unifies
point, box, and mask prompts within one model, allowing users to choose the level of spatial control
while benefiting from joint multi-granularity training.

Methodology
Our method presents a unified multi-granularity framework for controllable image-to-3D part gen-
eration, accommodating diverse geometric user inputs, such as point prompts, 2D bounding boxes,
and 2D masks, within a single model. Built upon the DiT architecture, FlexPart leverages a Gated
Adaptive Layer Normalization (Gated AdaLN) mechanism to inject these geometric conditions,
enabling unified modeling of heterogeneous inputs with varying precision. To further support this
versatility, we incorporate a geometric guidance mechanism to maintain robustness under weak
supervision. The overall architecture is illustrated in Fig. 2.

our setup, we treat individual parts as distinct samples. A batch contains B parts, each represented by
L latent tokens, collectively forming a hidden state tensor H ∈RB×L×D.

generation under varying levels of geometric guidance.

Implementation Details and Metrics
5.1
Implementation Details
We use a pretrained 3D VAE from TripoSG [16] to obtain part latents. Each part is encoded into latent
tokens following 3DShape2VecSet [47]. Following PartCrafter [20], we initialize the generative
backbone with pretrained TripoSG [16] weights and fine-tune it using the following two-stage training
strategy. We train FlexPart on a composite dataset derived from Objaverse [7] and PartVerseXL [8].
Stage 1 targets objects with 1–8 parts, each encoded with 512 tokens. To ensure robustness against
suboptimal placement, we randomly sample points within masks and apply a 0.5 dropout rate to
both boxes and masks. Stage 2 scales to 2–16 parts using 1024 tokens per part to handle varying
granularities. In this stage, we set the dropout probability to 0.1 for points and 0.7 for masks, similar
to [12, 31]. For bounding boxes, we implement a mixed dropping strategy with a probability of 0.25
for full dropout, and 0.25 for partial masking applied to half of the parts. Training is conducted on 8
NVIDIA H20 GPUs with a batch size of 256, using learning rates of 1 × 10−4 and 5 × 10−5 for the
two stages, respectively.
5.2
Datasets and Metrics
To assess generalization, we conduct evaluations on a held-out test set from PartVerseXL [8] and
PartObjaverse-Tiny [44], ensuring strict disjointness from the training set. For each dataset, we select
the view that maximizes the product of visible pixel count and number of visible parts, ensuring
sufficient part coverage.
Whole Mesh Evaluation Metrics. We first assemble the generated parts into a complete mesh and
evaluate overall geometric quality using Chamfer Distance (CD) and F-Score, where F-Score is
computed with a distance threshold of 0.1. Semantic consistency between the generated mesh and
the input image is measured using ULIP [41].
Part-level Evaluation Metrics. A primary challenge in evaluating parts is that the number of
generated components often differs from the ground truth. To ensure a fair comparison, we first filter
out ground truth parts that are fully occluded in the input image. We apply CD-based Hungarian
matching between the remaining ground-truth parts and generated parts to obtain one-to-one corre-
spondences. We compute part-level metrics only over matched pairs; unmatched ground-truth and
generated parts are excluded without additional penalties. Based on these matched pairs, we report
Part CD to measure geometric deviation. Furthermore, we evaluate spatial alignment using part-level
bounding-box IoU (IoUB) for coarse extent, and surface-based voxel IoU (IoUV ) for fine-grained
overlap. To handle non-watertight geometry, we define occupancy based on the distance field rather
than interior filling. Specifically, we use a voxel grid with a pitch of hvox = 0.03, where a voxel is
considered occupied if its distance to the nearest mesh surface is less than or equal to hvox. Unless
otherwise stated, benchmark box inputs are derived from ground-truth masks, and points are sampled
from the corresponding mask regions.
Detailed dataset construction and benchmark preparation are provided in App. A.

Experiments
6.1
Main Results
We compare FlexPart against six state-of-the-art methods, including two holistic mesh generation
models [16, 40] and four native part-based generation approaches [8, 20, 35, 46].
Part-level Mesh Quality. We first evaluate the geometric fidelity of individual parts (Tab. 1) to
assess controllable part generation. For comparison with whole-mesh generation methods, we employ
SAM3D [45] to project multi-view masks onto their generated meshes for part segmentation. This
post-hoc lifting process often introduces geometric inconsistencies, significantly degrading part-level
accuracy. In contrast, FlexPart directly infers compact part geometry. Under the same mask condition,
our method outperforms OmniPart by a large margin, reducing Part CD on PartVerseXL by 10.5%.
This improvement is consistent with the benefits of direct part generation: OmniPart uses intermediate
axis-aligned 3D boxes, which can enclose substantial empty space around non-axis-aligned shapes.
Crucially, FlexPart maintains robust performance across varying input granularities. Relaxing the
condition from masks to boxes yields stable quality, with Part CD degrading by merely 3.5% on
PartVerseXL. Even under these weaker priors, FlexPart achieves better Part CD and Part IoUB than
structurally unconstrained methods like PartPacker. FullPart is excluded here due to its reliance on
3D bounding boxes. A fairer evaluation under controlled settings is provided in Sec. 6.2.
Whole Mesh Quality. Complementing our superior part-level performance, we further assess the
global quality of the assembled meshes. As shown in Tab. 2, FlexPart achieves strong and consistent
mesh-level quality across both benchmarks. On PartVerseXL, we achieve a Mean CD of 0.1418,
surpassing OmniPart by 8%, indicating that our improvements in local part precision translate into
better global geometric accuracy. On PartObjaverse-Tiny, while some specialized methods achieve
marginally better results, FlexPart remains highly competitive. These results show that FlexPart
supports flexible part control while maintaining competitive whole-mesh quality.

Analysis and Ablation Study
To investigate the architecture design, we conduct ablation studies on PartVerseXL unless otherwise
specified. Default settings are marked in gray .
Effect of Multi-granularity Synergy. Tab. 3 demonstrates that our framework achieves deep cross-
modal synergy instead of a naive combination of conditions. Specifically, stronger geometric priors
effectively guide weaker ones during training. Our joint model improves Point only inference by
7.9% in IoUV and 6.1% in CD because it internalizes precise boundary constraints from dense inputs.
Furthermore, joint training consistently outperforms single-modality models across all evaluation
settings. For example, under mask conditioning, the unified model reduces Part CD from 0.3224
for the mask-only training baseline to 0.2938. These results confirm that FlexPart learns integrated
semantics in a shared space. This formulation provides a versatile solution that maintains high
geometric fidelity across diverse interaction modes. Fig. 4 illustrates robust, semantically consistent
results across prompt granularities.

Effect of Asymmetric Geometric Guidance.
Building upon the observed multi-granularity syn-
ergy, we evaluate the proposed Asymmetric Geomet-
ric Guidance by ablating the auxiliary alignment loss.
As shown in Tab. 4, it significantly improves perfor-
mance under sparse point prompts, yielding gains
of 7.9% in Part IoUB and 5.0% in Part IoUV over
the baseline. This indicates that OT-based alignment
effectively transfers structural priors from dense an-
notations to regularize learning from sparse inputs.
Performance under mask conditions remains comparable, suggesting positive cross-granularity trans-
fer without conflict. The guidance operates only during training, with no inference overhead.
