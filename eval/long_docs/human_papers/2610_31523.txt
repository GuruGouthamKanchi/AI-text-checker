Biparametric Prostate MRI
Claudia Giardina[0000−0002−2670−7069], Montse Pardàs[0000−0002−5861−6356], and
Verónica Vilaplana[0000−0001−6924−9961]

Abstract. Synthetic biparametric MRI (bpMRI) may help alleviate the
limited availability of large and representative prostate MRI datasets for
deep learning applications. In this work, we present a conditional 3D dif-
fusion framework for anatomy-conditioned synthesis of complete prostate
bpMRI from anatomical masks. The framework supports sequential syn-
thesis, in which T2w and diffusion modalities are generated by separately
trained models, and joint synthesis, in which all bpMRI modalities are
generated simultaneously within a single model. Five configurations were
trained using PI-CAI, FastMRI Prostate, and PROSTATEx datasets to
investigate different conditioning schemes, target modalities, and synthe-
sis strategies. Evaluation using distribution-based metrics, paired-image
similarity, and qualitative assessment demonstrated the feasibility of
both synthesis pathways. Joint synthesis achieved competitive distribu-
tional fidelity while generating complete bpMRI, whereas direct synthesis
of ADC and computed high-b-value DWI outperformed their derivation
from synthetic diffusion-weighted images.

Introduction
Prostate cancer is one of the most common malignancies in men, with more
than 1.4 million new cases estimated worldwide in 2022 [21]. Biparametric MRI
(bpMRI) is central to the non-invasive assessment of clinically significant prostate
cancer (csPCa) within the PI-RADS framework [19]. A bpMRI examination
comprises T2-weighted imaging (T2w) and diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI)
acquired at multiple b-values. From these acquisitions, quantitative apparent
diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps can be derived, while very-high-b-value DWI
may be acquired directly or computed from lower-b-value images.
BpMRI is increasingly used in deep learning applications such as lesion de-
tection, segmentation, and csPCa classification [7, 4, 6]. However, model perfor-
mance and generalization depend on the availability of large and representa-
tive annotated datasets [3]. This limitation has motivated increasing interest
in prostate MRI synthesis as a means of augmenting existing datasets with

Early work on prostate MRI synthesis explored the generation of individual
MRI modalities and paired diffusion images using 2D diffusion models [16]. More
recently, several studies have extended prostate MRI synthesis to 3D volumes, in-
cluding anatomy-conditioned T2w generation for segmentation augmentation [1,
8], clinically conditioned T2w synthesis [9], and multimodal generation of T2w,
high-b-value DWI, and ADC [10].
Collectively, these studies demonstrate the feasibility of prostate MRI syn-
thesis using diffusion models. However, anatomy-conditioned generation of com-
plete 3D bpMRI from explicit prostate-zone and lesion masks remains largely
unexplored. Furthermore, it remains unclear whether the diffusion component
of bpMRI should be synthesized at the acquisition level by generating diffusion-
weighted images and subsequently deriving ADC and computed high-b-value
DWI, or directly at the biomarker level by generating ADC and computed high-
b-value DWI themselves.
To investigate these alternative synthesis strategies, we present a conditional
3D diffusion framework for anatomy-conditioned generation of complete prostate
bpMRI from anatomical masks. The framework supports both sequential synthe-
sis, in which T2w and diffusion modalities are generated by separately trained
models, and joint synthesis, in which all bpMRI modalities are generated simulta-
neously within a single model. It further supports both acquired diffusion targets
(LBV and HBV) and diffusion-derived targets (ADC and cHBV), enabling the
evaluation of alternative routes for complete bpMRI synthesis. The framework
builds upon Med-DDPM [5] and incorporates direct x0 prediction, anisotropy-
aware resolution changes, and flexible conditioning and target configurations.
Five model configurations were trained using PI-CAI, FastMRI Prostate, and
PROSTATEx data to evaluate different conditioning schemes, target modali-
ties, and synthesis strategies for complete bpMRI generation.
2
Methodology
2.1
Framework Overview
The proposed framework is based on a conditional 3D diffusion architecture
derived from Med-DDPM [5]. The framework employs a 3D U-Net denoiser in
which the conditioning information c is concatenated channel-wise with the noisy
sample xt at each reverse-diffusion step, yielding the input ˜xt = [xt, c].
The framework introduces three modifications. First, the denoiser directly
predicts the clean target volume, ˆx0 = fθ([xt, c], t), instead of the added noise.
Preliminary T2w synthesis experiments indicated substantially improved distri-
butional fidelity compared with noise prediction. This formulation was therefore
adopted in all experiments.
Second, anisotropy-aware depth regulation (Fig. 1a) limits through-plane
downsampling when the axial depth reaches a predefined minimum, preventing

Third, the conditioning and output configurations extend beyond the origi-
nal mask-conditioned formulation. The condition c may consist of the anatomical
mask alone (Fig. 1b) or of the mask concatenated with a T2w volume, while the
target x0 may contain either a single modality or multiple modalities. These
configurations enable both sequential synthesis, in which T2w and diffusion tar-
gets are generated by separately trained models, and joint synthesis, in which
all bpMRI modalities are generated simultaneously within a single model.
2.2
Datasets and Image Preprocessing
Three public datasets were used: PI-CAI [17], comprising 1,500 bpMRI studies
with T2w, ADC, and computed high-b-value DWI (cHBV); PROSTATEx [14],
comprising 346 mpMRI studies; and FastMRI Prostate [18], comprising 312
bpMRI studies. PI-CAI provides prostate-zone and lesion annotations, while cor-
responding prostate and lesion masks were obtained for FastMRI and PROSTA-
TEx. T2w, ADC, and cHBV were used from PI-CAI, whereas T2w, LBV, and
HBV were used from FastMRI and PROSTATEx. FastMRI and PROSTATEx
were preprocessed using QP-Prostate [15], including image registration and qual-
ity control, yielding 235 and 281 usable studies, respectively. For PI-CAI, ADC
and cHBV were registered to T2w using Elastix [12], resulting in 1,158 usable
studies. All volumes were resampled to 0.5 × 0.5 × 3.0 mm, prostate-centred
cropped to 192 × 192 × 20 voxels, and intensity-normalized to [−1, 1].

2.3
Experimental Design
In prostate bpMRI, DWI is acquired at multiple diffusion weightings, or b-values,
typically including a low-b-value image (LBV, approximately 50–100 s/mm2) and
a high-b-value image (HBV, approximately 800–1000 s/mm2). These acquisitions
can be used to derive additional diffusion representations, including apparent
diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps and computed high-b-value DWI (cHBV, ap-
proximately 1400 s/mm2). This motivates two alternative diffusion synthesis
strategies: generation of the acquired diffusion images (LBV and HBV), from
which ADC and cHBV can subsequently be derived, and direct generation of
the diffusion-derived representations ADC and cHBV.
Each diffusion target configuration was evaluated using both synthesis strate-
gies supported by the proposed framework: sequential synthesis and joint syn-
thesis. In sequential synthesis, a dedicated mask-conditioned model first gener-
ates T2w. A second model, conditioned on both the anatomical mask and the
T2w volume, then generates either LBV/HBV or ADC/cHBV. During complete
bpMRI synthesis, the generated T2w volume is used to condition the second-
stage model. In joint synthesis, a single mask-conditioned model generates T2w
together with either LBV/HBV or ADC/cHBV. The resulting experimental de-
sign comprises five model configurations, summarized in Table 1.
M1 was trained using PI-CAI and FastMRI (1615/120 train/test cases), M2
and M4 using PROSTATEx and FastMRI (476/40), and M3 and M5 using PI-
CAI only (1146/12), according to the availability of the target modalities.
2.4
Training Details
All models were trained using Adam, scale-shift normalization, 250 diffusion
steps, and the direct x0-prediction objective. M1 and M2 used 64 initial U-Net
channels (166.8M parameters), whereas M3–M5 used 96 channels (375.2M pa-
rameters). Learning rates were set to 2 × 10−5 for M1, M4, and M5 and to
1 × 10−5 for M2 and M3. All remaining optimization settings followed Med-
DDPM [5]. To soften sharp boundaries in the anatomical condition, anisotropic
Gaussian smoothing (σz = 0.4, σxy = 1.0) was applied stochastically to con-
ditioning masks during training, with probabilities of 40% for M1 and 50%

for M2–M5. Training minimized a voxel-wise L1 loss. For multi-target mod-

balance learning across modalities.
2.5
Evaluation
Distribution-based evaluation: The alignment between the real and syn-
thetic image distributions was evaluated using FID [11], KID [2], and Precision
and Recall [13]. Metrics were computed on the 20 axial slices of each volume,
treated as independent samples. The synthetic volumes used for this evaluation
were generated from an augmented set of PI-CAI anatomical masks. Augmenta-
tion was designed to increase anatomical variability and balance lesion-positive
and lesion-negative cases, and included prostate-level transformations (flipping
and scaling) as well as lesion-level transformations (flipping, translation, and
transfer between anatomically similar prostates). The resulting set comprised
1,500 masks. Synthetic volumes were generated using the corresponding sequen-
tial and joint synthesis pipelines. In sequential synthesis, T2w volumes were
first generated from the augmented masks and subsequently used, together with
the masks, to generate LBV/HBV or ADC/cHBV. In joint synthesis, all target
modalities were generated directly from the augmented masks. For each modal-
ity, synthetic volumes were compared with a size-matched real reference set:
1,500 real and synthetic PI-CAI T2w volumes, 1,158 real and synthetic PI-CAI
ADC and cHBV volumes, and 516 real and synthetic FastMRI/PROSTATEx
LBV and HBV volumes.
Paired-image evaluation: PSNR and SSIM [20] were computed volume-wise
between each generated volume and its matched real target for all test cases.
Metrics were computed within the prostate region and averaged across datasets
when applicable. For models conditioned only on anatomical masks (M1, M4, and
M5), these metrics should be interpreted as measures of case-specific anatomical
consistency rather than reconstruction fidelity.
3
Results
For T2w synthesis (Table 2), the joint models M4 and M5 achieved distribution-
based performance comparable to that of the dedicated T2w model M1. M1 ob-
tained the lowest FID and KID values, while both M4 and M5 achieved higher
Precision and Recall. These results indicate that jointly generating T2w together
with additional diffusion modalities does not substantially compromise the real-
ism or diversity of the synthesized T2w images.
The qualitative examples in Fig. 2 illustrate differences in the generated T2w
appearances. M1 generally produced sharper anatomical boundaries and more
detailed T2w texture, whereas the jointly generated T2w images from M4 and
M5 appeared slightly smoother while remaining anatomically consistent with the
conditioning masks.

The examples in Fig. 3 show that both models reproduced the expected
contrast differences between LBV and HBV while preserving prostate morphol-
ogy. In some cases, M2 produced lesion regions with sharper, mask-conforming
boundaries than those observed in real DWI, suggesting a degree of mask im-

For ADC and cHBV synthesis (Table 3), the sequential model M3 and the
joint model M5 achieved very similar distribution-based performance. ADC met-
rics were nearly identical, while M3 showed a slight advantage for cHBV, achiev-
ing lower FID and KID values and higher Recall. These results indicate that
jointly generating T2w, ADC, and cHBV does not substantially compromise the
quality of the diffusion-derived modalities.
A clearer difference emerged when comparing direct synthesis with the acquired-
DWI pathway. ADC and cHBV computed from synthetic LBV and HBV gener-
ated by M4 showed substantially worse distribution-based performance than the
directly synthesized modalities produced by M3 and M5. This degradation was
observed across all metrics except cHBV Precision, which was slightly higher
for the derived images, suggesting that errors introduced during LBV and HBV
synthesis propagate to the derived ADC and cHBV representations.

Conclusion
This study presented a conditional 3D DDPM framework for anatomy-conditioned
synthesis of prostate bpMRI, supporting both sequential and joint generation of
T2w and diffusion modalities from anatomical masks. The results demonstrate
the feasibility of both synthesis strategies. Joint synthesis showed modality-
specific differences in distributional fidelity relative to dedicated models while
generating complete bpMRI examinations within a single model. For diffusion-
derived modalities, direct synthesis of ADC and cHBV consistently achieved
better distribution-based performance than derivation from synthetic LBV and
HBV, suggesting that errors introduced during diffusion-image synthesis may
propagate to the derived representations.
Although the quantitative evaluation and qualitative examples support the
plausibility of the generated images, further validation is required to determine
whether diagnostically relevant characteristics are preserved and whether the
generated data provide clinical value. Future work will therefore include expert
radiological assessment and evaluation in downstream tasks such as csPCa clas-
sification, lesion detection, and prostate segmentation.
