BeNice! Closing Looping’s Inference-Memory Gap via KV-Sharing

Abstract
Looped architectures scale quality while keeping parameter count fixed by repeatedly passing
activations through the same block of layers.
During inference, each recurrence generates
independent KV-caches, causing memory to grow linearly. To address this challenge, prior
work has proposed KV-sharing (i.e., sharing KV-caches across depth), finding that, in some
cases, the KV-cache of the final recurrence can be shared with the earlier recurrences without
quality loss, a property we denote as KV-shareability. However, the mechanisms underlying
KV-shareability remain poorly understood, with even identically trained looped architectures
not consistently exhibiting it. In this paper, we study how KV-shareability can be enabled in
looped models. We propose a two-stage method: in the first stage, we use stochastic depth during
pretraining, which induces contractive residual dynamics that drive representations toward a
common limit across recurrences. In the second stage, we propose BeNice!, a post-training
method that broadcasts the final recurrence’s KV-cache into the first recurrence, training it to
produce compatible residuals while preserving the original model’s quality. As a result, BeNice!
enables KV-shareability while matching standard finetuning’s unshared validation perplexity.
Using BeNice!, we show that KV-sharing is complementary to alternative KV-compression
methods, composing with memory-efficient sequence mixers (e.g., multi-latent attention, Kimi
delta attention) and low-precision KV-cache quantization (e.g., fp8, mxfp8, nvfp4). Finally, in
simulated low-memory, long-context inference, KV-sharing improves cache hit rates by up to
31.4%, increases throughput by up to 23.6%, and reduces p90 time-to-first-token by up to 83.6%.

Introduction
As inference deployments move to the edge [43, 45] and take up a large portion of compute [67],
looped architectures create a new, predictable avenue to scale quality while keeping parameter
count fixed [20, 41, 50]. Unfortunately, while looped architectures keep the memory overhead of
parameters capped, they still create KV-caches at each recurrence [19, 50, 80], causing memory
overhead to still grow linearly with recurrence count at inference time. One potential direction
to reduce inference-memory overhead is KV-sharing (i.e., sharing KV-caches across recurrences).
Specifically, some recent looped models have been shown to support reuse of the final recurrence’s
KV-cache for all prior recurrences with minimal impact to quality [20, 21], a property we term
KV-shareability (Figure 1 [right]).
Unfortunately, the mechanisms underlying KV-shareability remain poorly understood, with more
recent work reporting significant quality degradation with KV-cache reuse [19, 80]. Moreover, even

In this paper, we hypothesize that contractive recurrent dynamics drive residual states toward fixed
points, whose agreement under shared and unshared KV-caches enables KV-shareability.
To encourage contractive residual dynamics, we train with stochastic depth, randomly sampling
depth throughout training (Figure 1 [left]). By requiring low-loss predictions across multiple depths,
stochastic depth discourages residual drift and promotes convergence across recurrences. Contractive
residual dynamics are what allow KV-shareability, which we formally analyze and empirically validate
by showing that replacing intermediate caches with the final cache perturbs the attention output by
a term bounded by the difference between their input residuals (see Section 3.1).
Unfortunately, even though stochastic depth creates contractive residual dynamics, it does not
guarantee that residual states converge to the same fixed point with and without KV-sharing.
Instead, KV-sharing sometimes introduces token-independent residual errors in the first recurrence,
shifting the model’s fixed point and harming downstream quality (see Section 3.2).
To enable KV-shareability, we pair stochastic depth pretraining with a targeted correction of the
residual errors introduced by KV-sharing. We propose a two-stage method: first, stochastic depth
pretraining to induce contractive residual dynamics; second, BeNice! to correct first recurrence
residual errors created from KV-sharing. Specifically, BeNice! jointly optimizes both the first
recurrence with KV-cache reuse and the final recurrence without reuse (Figure 1 [middle]), balancing
KV-shareability and base-model quality. To further study KV-sharing with other KV-compression
techniques, we extended BeNice! to memory-efficient sequence mixers, including multi-latent
attention (MLA) [13] and Kimi delta attention (KDA) [65], by reusing the final recurrence’s latent
or state, respectively. Overall, BeNice! unlocks near-lossless KV-cache reuse across recurrences,
facilitating predictable quality scaling using extended looping [50] while keeping parameter count
and KV-cache memory constant as depth grows.
We evaluate KV-shareability and BeNice! on end-to-end quality, compatibility with other KV
compression methods, and how it accelerates inference workloads:
• End-to-End Quality. BeNice! improves shared quality by up to 8.35 and 4.56 points for Core
and Core-extended [38], respectively. Compared with standard finetuning, BeNice! improves
validation perplexity by 9.75 points when sharing, while matching quality in the unshared case.
• Complementary to other KV Compression Methods. To evaluate KV-sharing with other
KV-compression methods, we study its composition with memory-efficient sequence mixers and
KV-quantization. We find that KV-sharing composes with both MLA and KDA sequence mixers
and low-precision KV-quantization (e.g., fp8, mxfp4, nvfp4).
• Accelerating Inference Workloads. Under simulated long-context inference traffic, we observe

Background
We first provide a brief background on prior looped models (Section 2.1) and the inference overhead
of language models (Section 2.2). Looped architectures have been widely explored in synthetic
problem solving tasks [7, 29, 31, 57, 70]; however, our work focuses on looped language models
[14, 20, 50, 80]. These models differ in where recurrence occur (pre-, mid, post-, or full-looping) [2],
how it is halted (i.e., explicit router [80] or implicitly within the residual [20, 50]), and how gradients
are computed (explicit or implicit backpropagation [5]). We study middle-looped models using
implicit halting and explicit differentiation, with an emphasis on how pretraining for fixed-point
convergence enables memory-efficient inference. An extended literature review is found in Section B.
2.1
Prior Looped Models
Most prior looped language models [20, 50, 80] follow a middle-looped structure sequentially composed
of an initial prelude block P, a middle recurrent block R, and a final coda block C. For an input
sequence s ∈Vn, computation proceeds sequentially as e = P(s), ht+1 = R(ht, e), p = C(hT ),
where a model is looped T times and h0 is initialized randomly (i.e., h0 ∼N(0, Iσ2); [20, 50]) or
with e (i.e., h0 ←e; [80]). Intuitively, P maps a sequence s into a latent embedding e ∈Rn×de,
R iteratively refines a hidden state ht ∈Rn×dh conditioned on e, and then C maps the final hT to
an output p ∈Rn×|V|. To condition R on e, input injection at each recurrence is often used (i.e.,
ht+1 = R(A · ht + Be), where A ∈Rdh×dh and B ∈Rdh×de; [20, 50, 73]), and following Prairie et al.
[50] is constrains ρ(A) < 1 where ρ(·) is the spectral radius, to prevent state explosion.
During training, prior works either sample depth stochastically [20, 50] or use adaptive computation
time (ACT) [23]. Specifically, Parcae [50] and Huginn [20] sample a depth T ∼Λ independently
per sequence/batch, where Λ is a distribution (e.g., Poisson) with mean µrec, training R to produce
meaningful representations across depths. Ouro [80] uses ACT: a learned router predicts a halting
probability at each recurrence, with computation stopping once the cumulative probability exceeds a
threshold [23]. We will show the advantages of training with stochastically sampled depth in this
work. Finally, to bound training memory, some models use truncated backpropagation through time
(TBPTT) [20, 50], propagating gradients through only the final µbwd < T recurrences.
2.2
Inference Overhead of Looped Language Models
We will study how memory overhead at inference scales with context length and model depth,
particularly within the scope of looped architectures [20, 50]. Under uniform numerical precision,
inference memory M for a P-parameter model, with L context length, D depth, and dkv KV
width is M = Mθ + Mkv, where model-weight memory is Mθ = O(P) and KV-cache memory is
Mkv = O(LDdkv). Beyond lowering numerical precision, prior work reduces Mkv overhead primarily
by shrinking per-layer footprint Ldkv (e.g., MLA [13], GQA [1], KDA [65]), leaving its linear
dependence on depth D unchanged. For a looped model evaluated with T recurrences, the effective
depth is D = DP + T · DR + DC. Although looping keeps Mθ fixed as T increases, conventional
inference stores a distinct KV cache for each recurrence, causing Mkv to grow linearly with T.

3
Understanding KV-shareability in Looped Models
In this section, we study KV-shareability in looped architectures. To evaluate KV-shareability, we
compare shared evaluations, which reuse the final-recurrence cache across all recurrences, against
unshared evaluations, which retain every recurrence’s cache. Details on reproducing accurate KV-
sharing inference setups for our benchmarks are provided in Section C.
We motivate our investigation by observing that KV-shareability is not universal to all looped models
(see Table 1). We establish that stochastic depth enables contractive recurrent dynamics, which
we formally analyze and empirically validate allows KV-shareability when fixed points agree under
shared and unshared KV-caches (Section 3.1). However, stochastic depth alone is insufficient to
guarantee agreement, leading to models not exhibiting KV-shareability (e.g., Parcae 1.3B in Table 1).
Analyzing failure cases, we find that KV-sharing creates a token-independent basis shift, causing
convergence to different fixed points and degraded quality under KV-sharing (Section 3.2).
3.1
Stochastic Depth Enables Contractive Residual Dynamics
In this section, we characterize how stochastic-depth training induces fixed-point convergence, driving
KV-caches near a common limit and correlating with the emergence of KV-shareability.
Setup.
We train Parcae-style [50] models from scratch at four scales (140M, 370M, 770M, and
1.3B), varying only whether recurrence depth is stochastic (i.e., randomly sampled during training)
or static (i.e., fixed throughout training). KV-shareability is evaluated on final models and never
trained for explicitly. Training and evaluation details are in Section G and Section C, respectively.

Learning to Share Across Recurrences
One way to induce KV-shareability is to train directly on KV-sharing rollouts (i.e., sequentially rolling
out KV-sharing dynamics token-by-token). However, this would be computationally infeasible and
slow during training. Alternatively, using our analysis in Section 3, we propose a two-stage method
to enable KV-shareability (as visualized in Figure 1). In the first stage, we follow our observations in
Section 3.1 and pretrain models with stochastic depth to induce contractive residual dynamics that
drive KV-caches to a common limit across recurrences (Section 4.1). In the second stage, we correct
the token-independent basis shift observed in Section 3.2 with BeNice! by optimizing both the first
recurrence with KV-cache reuse and the standard final recurrence without reuse (Section 4.2).
4.1
Stochastic-Depth Pretraining: Enabling Contractive Recurrent Dynamics
In the first stage of our method, we train with stochastic depth, driving KV-caches to a common
shareable limit across recurrences and setting the groundwork for KV-shareability based on Section 3.1.
Specifically, following Prairie et al. [50], we utilize per-sequence stochastic depth training, assigning
a different depth for each sequence in a batch. This sampling structure was empirically observed
to reduce loss spikes in prior work. We further follow Prairie et al. [50], sampling per-sequence
depth from T ∼Λ(µrec), where Λ is a Poisson distribution with mean µrec. Lastly, to keep memory
bounded during pretraining, we use truncated backpropagation through recurrences, with a backprop
depth of µbwd = ⌊µrec
2 ⌋[50].

Results
We evaluate end-to-end quality of finetuning methods for enabling KV-shareability (Section 5.1), its
composition with other KV compression techniques (Section 5.2), and its latency and throughput
gains in memory-bound setups (Section 5.3). Overall, we find BeNice! enables KV-shareability,
improving shared Core and Core-Extended [38] quality by up to 8.35 and 4.56 points, respectively.
Furthermore, BeNice! is complementary to memory-efficient sequence mixers (e.g., MLA [13],
KDA [65]) and KV-quantization. Lastly, in synthetic memory-constrained inference workloads,
KV-shareability improves cache-hit rate by up to 31.4% (8.1 percentage points), increases throughput
by up to 23.6%, and reduces p90 TTFT by up to 83.6%.

achieves comparable results to standard finetuning, while enabling KV-shareability.
