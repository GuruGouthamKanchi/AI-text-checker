Abstract
Bashkir is a low-resource agglutinative language written in Cyrillic and remains poorly represented in
the ecosystem of modern large language models. This work presents an open resource suite for Bashkir
and a systematic benchmark of large-language-model adaptation methods: the Bashkir Web Corpus
(71,567 documents, 46.9 million tokens, 16 sources) and three labelled datasets for text classification:
binary (16,994 documents), multiclass (17,897 documents, 19 categories) and multilabel (22,318
documents, 14 labels); the benchmark is conducted on the first two. Experiments are conducted
on eight architectures in the 82M–8B parameter range (DistilGPT2, GPT-2 base, GPT-2 medium,
Phi-3-mini, Mistral-7B, YandexGPT-5-Lite-8B, mBERT, XLM-R) and three groups of adaptation
strategies: full fine-tuning, QLoRA and in-context learning (zero-shot and few-shot); the benefit of
continual pretraining (CPT) on the Bashkir corpus is additionally investigated.
Four results constitute the main contribution of the work. First, QLoRA adaptation of 3.8–8B
generative models outperforms full fine-tuning of encoder-only baselines on binary classification:
YandexGPT-5-Lite-8B with CPT reaches a macro-F1 of 0.748 versus 0.653 for XLM-R and 0.650 for
mBERT; in a pairwise comparison of accuracy for the step6 configurations (0.737 versus 0.659 and
0.652), the difference between QLoRA adaptation and the encoder-only baseline is significant after
Holm correction for 15 comparisons (𝑝Holm < 0.001). Second, YandexGPT-5-Lite-8B significantly
outperforms Mistral-7B and Phi-3-mini on multiclass classification, BPB and native-speaker ratings
(mean 3.28 versus 2.52 and 2.22; 𝑛= 100; annotator agreement 𝛼= 0.69–0.77; 𝑝Holm < 0.05);
zero-shot ICL shows a numerical advantage (0.58–0.68 versus 0.36–0.51 macro-F1), but no formal
test was conducted for it; on binary classification, the numerical advantage of YandexGPT-5-Lite-8B
is not statistically confirmed within the QLoRA group (𝑝Holm ≥0.084). Third, the effect of CPT
depends on both the base model and the task: on perplexity, CPT is significant for all three models but
with opposite signs — it improves Phi-3-mini (Δ = +0.58) and YandexGPT-5-Lite-8B (Δ = +0.33)
and worsens Mistral-7B (Δ = −1.15, 𝑝Holm < 0.001); on binary classification, by contrast, CPT
gives the largest gain precisely for Mistral-7B (+4.0 macro-F1 points). Fourth, tokeniser fertility
on Bashkir varies by a factor of 2.67 (from 2.92 to 7.80) and is consistent with adaptation quality,
whereas perplexity under different tokenisers is unsuitable for cross-model comparison.
Practical recommendations: under a limited computational budget, preference should be given to
models with a Cyrillic tokeniser and Russian-language pretraining; for assessing generation quality in
a low-resource language, direct scoring by LLM judges reproduces native-speaker ratings for grammar
and coherence (𝜌= 0.33–0.46) but not for language correctness (𝜌= 0.16, 𝑝= 0.11); native-speaker
evaluation remains necessary. All artefacts — the corpus, datasets, experiment code and trained
adapters — are released under open licences.

Introduction
Bashkir belongs to the Turkic language family and is one of the state languages of the Republic of Bashkortostan.
Despite this, Bashkir has an extremely limited digital presence: the volume of machine-readable texts, annotated corpora
and pretrained language models is substantially smaller than for languages with a high digital presence and than for a
number of closely related Turkic languages.
Over the past few years, large language models have made a qualitative leap: growth in the number of parameters, the
transition to instruction tuning, the emergence of parameter-efficient adaptation methods and open models have made
large language models the main instrument of natural language processing com [2026], Burnashev et al. [2026]. This
development, however, is unevenly distributed across languages. The overwhelming majority of modern models are
trained on English, which leads to digital inequality: languages with a limited digital presence — including Bashkir —
are effectively excluded from the ecosystem of modern language technologies.
This inequality manifests itself at three levels. First, tokenisers of multilingual models fragment agglutinative word
forms into excessively long subsequences of subtokens, which reduces processing quality Eyupoglu [2019], Grechachin
[2018]. Second, the absence of large open corpora limits the possibilities of both pretraining from scratch and continual
training Khudiakova [2025]. Third, existing linguistic resources for Bashkir were created within a paradigm oriented
towards search and aggregation of data rather than machine learning Sirazitdinov [2025].
The digitisation of the Bashkir language began in the 2000s and is associated primarily with the creation of the Machine
Fund of the Bashkir Language — an integrated information system developed at the Institute of History, Language
and Literature, Ufa Federal Research Centre, Russian Academy of Sciences (IIL UFRC RAS) since 2006 Sirazitdinov
and Buskunbaeva [2010], Sirazitdinov [2025]. The structure of the Fund includes a general word list, lexicographic,
grammatical and experimental-phonetic databases, catalogues of manuscript and early printed books, a dialectological
database, and corpus databases of prose, journalistic and folklore texts Sirazitdinov [2025]. The total number of
linguistic data databases exceeds 75. These resources became the basis for subsequent corpus and applied developments
Sirazitdinov [2024].
However, the architecture of the Machine Fund, developed on the basis of the Oracle relational database management
system, is oriented towards search and aggregation of linguistic information and is not intended for training neural
network models. The resulting gap between accumulated linguistic data and modern machine-learning methods
determined the need to create open corpora suitable for pretraining and fine-tuning transformer architectures.
In parallel, a strand emerged in Turkic studies concerned with the application of natural language processing methods to
low-resource languages. Studies show that transfer of knowledge from closely related languages, multilingual training
and parameter-efficient fine-tuning make it possible to achieve acceptable quality even with limited data volumes
Saykhunov and Suleymanov [2019], Khusainov and Suleymanov [2021], Mirzakhalov et al. [2021], Gatiatullin et al.
[2024], Suleymanov et al. [2026]. At the same time, until recently there were no systematic studies for Bashkir
comparing different strategies for adapting large language models.
The relevance of this work is determined by three factors. First, Bashkir remains a low-resource language, and existing
open corpora have a limited volume and source imbalance Khudiakova [2025]. Second, parameter-efficient fine-tuning
methods that have proved themselves in other languages com [2026], Burnashev et al. [2026], Arabov et al. [2026a] have
not been systematically tested on Bashkir material. Third, practical applications — machine translation Karpov [2026],
Maxim [2026], Tyurin [2026], speech recognition and synthesis Radford et al. [2022], Orel et al. [2023], Bekarystankyzy
et al. [2024], text classification Smetanina et al. [2020], Arabov et al. [2026a,b] — require reproducible benchmarks and
open datasets, which remain insufficient for Bashkir.
This study aims to fill this gap. The work presents three groups of artefacts and results. The first group is resources:
the open Bashkir Web Corpus with 46.9 million tokens, as well as three annotated datasets for binary, multiclass and
multilabel classification. The second group is empirical results: the application of QLoRA to medium-scale generative
models, an assessment of continual pretraining on the Bashkir corpus as a preliminary stage before downstream
adaptation, a comparison of in-context learning strategies (zero-shot and few-shot) with parameter updates, and full
fine-tuning of small architectures as a baseline. The third group is analysis: measurement of tokeniser fertility and
validation of LLM judges against ratings by native Bashkir speakers. Experiments were conducted on eight large language
model architectures in the range from 82M to 8B parameters: three small models of the GPT-2 family (DistilGPT2,
GPT-2 base, GPT-2 medium), three medium-scale generative models (Phi-3-mini, Mistral-7B, YandexGPT-5-Lite-8B)

The study addresses four research questions. RQ1: How does QLoRA adaptation of generative models of 3,8–8B

related to adaptation quality, and does it explain the gap between models with a Cyrillic vocabulary and a BPE

criteria remain inaccessible to automatic evaluation?
The work is organised as follows. Section 2 contains a review of the literature on the digitisation of the Bashkir language,
corpus linguistics, the morphology of agglutinative languages, PEFT and the application of PEFT to Turkic languages.
Section 3 describes the corpus, datasets, models, adaptation strategies, hyperparameters and metrics. Section 4 presents
results on language modelling, continual pretraining, binary and multiclass classification, in-context learning, generation
quality and tokeniser analysis. Section 5 discusses the factors behind the superiority of YandexGPT-5-Lite-8B, the
selective effect of CPT, the divergence between LLM judges and native speakers, the role of tokenisation and practical
recommendations. Section 6 formulates limitations, and Section 7 concludes and outlines directions for further work.
2
Related Work
2.1
Digitisation of the Bashkir Language and the Formation of Linguistic Resources
The first works describing the process of digitising the Bashkir language date from the early 2010s. The article
Sirazitdinov and Buskunbaeva [2010] describes in detail the experience of creating national font layouts, the integration
of the Bashkir language into operating systems together with Microsoft, and the conceptual foundations and launch of
the information system “Machine Fund of the Bashkir Language”. For modern natural language processing, this work is
a historical and methodological source explaining the origin and initial architecture of the main linguistic resource.
A generalising article is Khisamitdinova [2016], which summarises the development of Bashkir philology in the
post-Soviet period. Particular attention is paid to the creation of the ten-volume “Academic Dictionary of the Bashkir
Language” and the conversion of its dictionary databases into digital format — the creation of electronic glossaries and
terminological data banks. These lexicographic developments subsequently became the basis for building morphological
analysers and automatic dictionaries.
The most complete description of the structure and functional capabilities of the Machine Fund is presented in Sirazitdinov
[2025]. The author describes the organisation of ten large sections of the Fund (general word list, lexicographic,
grammatical, experimental-phonetic databases, catalogues of manuscript and early printed books, dialectological
database, corpus databases). For natural language processing tasks, this material is the main source for understanding
how the annotation of source data is organised and where aggregated characteristics are extracted from.
A retrospective analysis of scientific and practical activity in the field of applied linguistics at IIL UFRC RAS over more
than 30 years is presented in Sirazitdinov [2024]. Over this period, an infrastructure was created for the functioning of
the national language in computer technologies, theoretical foundations for an automatic morphological analyser, and
phonetic, lexical and full-text linguistic databases, which made it possible to develop the information systems “Machine
Fund of the Bashkir Language” and “Terminological Data Bank”.
2.2
Corpus Linguistics of the Bashkir Language
Practical aspects of creating corpora of the Bashkir language are considered in a number of works. In Ishmukhametova
[2014], the experience of creating a subcorpus of literary texts of the Machine Fund is described: issues of data cleaning,
tokenisation and formats for encoding metatextual annotation (genre, author, year) are considered. These aspects are
fundamentally important for selecting and filtering quality data during pretraining of transformer models.
In Buskunbaeva et al. [2018], using the material of the folklore corpus, types of one-member sentences in the proverbial
fund of the Bashkir language are described: the structural classification of proverbial constructions and the principles of
their annotation in the corpus are considered. This material is important for developing syntactic analysers and for
understanding how the annotation of folklore texts in Bashkir corpora is organised.
The dialectological aspect of corpus linguistics is presented in Sirazitdinov et al. [2016], which describes an applied
component of the Machine Fund — an integrated Dialectological Database consisting of a lexical subfund, a textological

Issues of code-switching using the material of the journalistic corpus are considered in Buskunbaeva [2022]. Structural

configuring acoustic models for Bashkir speech recognition.
Finally, the Bashqort Raw Corpus dataset Khudiakova [2025] is a collection of Bashkir texts intended for continual
training of large language models. The resource is published on the Hugging Face platform and is one of the few open
corpora of the Bashkir language.
2.3
Lexicography, Orthography and Formal Grammar
The transition of classical lexicography into the digital environment is considered in Yagafarova [2015]: the structure,
strengths and weaknesses of existing electronic and online Bashkir dictionaries are analysed. In solving natural language
processing tasks, these materials are used to develop automatic parsers, electronic glossaries and machine translation
systems.
A fundamental classification of the principles of nomination and thematic groups of Bashkir vocabulary is presented in
the doctoral dissertation Yagafarova [2019]. In IT developments, this structure is used to design Bashkir WordNet-like
networks, build semantic ontologies, knowledge graphs and named entity extraction systems.
The principles of creating the information and reference portal on Bashkir orthography Belemle.ru are described
in Yagafarova [2025]. For developers, the material is valuable for its description of standardised rules underlying
spell-checking algorithms, automatic correction of typos and normalisation of Bashkir text before it is fed into neural
network models.
The formalisation of Bashkir syntax from the standpoint of computer modelling is presented in Sirazitdinov [2021]: the
author identifies strict models and schemes of verbal government using the material of generalised-personal proverbs.
In natural language processing tasks, the formalisation of grammar makes it possible to specify rules for accurate
semantic-syntactic parsing of text constructions.
2.4
Applied Platforms and Services
The software architecture of the Bashkortsoft ecosystem is considered in Gataullin and Mukhametov [2022]: modules
for intelligent text analysis, an API for integrating Bashkir dictionaries into third-party IT solutions, algorithms for
automatic spell-checking and normalisation of noisy user input are described. This material is important as an example
of implementing applied text preprocessing pipelines before feeding data into neural networks.
The process of transferring scientific databases of the Machine Fund into commercial and publicly accessible web
interfaces of the Bashkortsoft project is described in Sirazitdinov and Buskunbaeva [2021]. Technical problems of
matching encodings, parsing large volumes of dictionary entries and optimising search queries for online translators
operating in real time are considered.
The concept of collecting open legally clean datasets for training large language models is presented in Gataullin [2024]:
problems of copyright in texts are analysed, and the integration of Bashkir translation on modern IT platforms is
described. The architecture of the Bashkorttele repository, including audiobooks, annotated Bashkir epics and works by
classical authors, is described in Yusupova and Gataullin [2026]. The data alignment format (each audio fragment is
strictly aligned with text in Bashkir and Russian) makes this work a methodological guide for training speech-to-text
systems.
Scaling of the Bashkorttele data repository is described in Kilsenbaev [2026]: the structure of a trilingual parallel spoken
corpus of 10,000 phrases is disclosed, as well as the integration of an archive of live Bashkir speech with a volume

2.5
Morphology of Agglutinative Languages
A two-level morphological description of the Bashkir language is presented in Eyupoglu [2019]: the description is
based on a lexicon of root words, implemented using XML and connected to the Nuve framework. The phonetic rules of
the Bashkir language are encoded using 41 two-level rules. This work is one of the few attempts at a formal description
of Bashkir morphology in terms of two-level morphology.
A network model of agglutinative morphology based on graph theory is proposed in Grechachin [2018]. The model is
implemented for modern Bashkir and for Old Turkic; on its basis, a morphological parser for the Old Turkic language
was created.
A neuro-symbolic approach to the morphology of Bashkir nouns is developed in Isangulov et al. [2025, 2026]. In
Isangulov et al. [2025], a hybrid method is presented that combines a large language model and a finite-state automaton
to ensure morphological correctness during text generation in agglutinative languages: the system automatically extracts
rules from corpus data, generates context-dependent regex rules and LEXC morphotactics; the integration of the
finite-state automaton into the language-model loop improved machine translation quality from BLEU 16.14 / ChrF
45.13 to BLEU 25.71 / ChrF 50.87 without retraining the translator. In Isangulov et al. [2026], the previous version of
the pipeline is extended: 69.60 % coverage was achieved and 174 of 250 forms were recognised on the same benchmark.
2.6
Turkic Languages: Corpora and Machine Translation
The principles of building multilingual Turkic corpora are considered in Saykhunov and Suleymanov [2019]: the
architecture of cross-lingual models for closely related languages is studied, and transfer learning algorithms are
proposed to improve the quality of machine translation and text classification in the low-resource Bashkir language.
A multilingual neural machine translation system for seven Turkic–Russian language pairs is presented in Khusainov
and Suleymanov [2021]: parallel corpora with a total volume of about 7.9 million sentence pairs were created, and for
the first time for Turkic languages, transfer learning experiments were conducted on a combined Russian–Turkic parallel
corpus.
A large-scale study of machine translation in the Turkic language family is presented in Mirzakhalov et al. [2021]:
a parallel corpus covering 22 Turkic languages was created, bilingual baseline models for 26 language pairs were
proposed, as well as new test sets in three different translation domains.
The toolkit of the “Turkic Morpheme” portal for data augmentation in low-resource machine translation is described in
Gatiatullin et al. [2024]: an augmentation method based on morphological translation showed an increase in machine
translation quality by the BLEU metric, especially effectively for closely related languages with similar grammar and
morphology.
Methods for creating machine translation systems for low-resource languages are considered in Suleymanov et al. [2026]:
an approach combining data augmentation and knowledge transfer is proposed; BERT is chosen as the architecture,
used both as an encoder and as a decoder. Parallel corpora with a total volume of about 30 million sentence pairs were
collected.
The Russian–Bashkir parallel corpus BorisTM [2025] contains 9.7 million sentence pairs filtered by semantic similarity.
The corpus combines real translations and synthetic data obtained by back-translation.
The application of parameter-efficient fine-tuning to the task of Russian–Bashkir machine translation is presented in
Karpov [2026]: fine-tuning of nllb-200-distilled-600M with LoRA on synthetic data achieved chrF++ 46.94 for Bashkir.
In Maxim [2026], adaptation of the Tencent HY-MT1.5-7B model with LoRA is described for correcting output from
Arabic script to Cyrillic in Russian–Kazakh and Russian–Bashkir pairs. In Tyurin [2026], a comparative study of
NLLB-200, M2M-100 and MarianMT architectures was conducted; the best system — a fine-tuned NLLB-200-1.3B
with exact-match extraction — reached chrF++ 52.67.
2.7
Speech Processing for Turkic Languages
The Whisper speech recognition model Radford et al. [2022] was adapted to Bashkir by reducing the vocabulary to
16,384 tokens. The Bashkort TTS dataset bas is the largest open dataset for speech synthesis in Bashkir: 62,852 audio
files, eight speakers and three speech styles.

Multilingual speech synthesis for Turkic languages using transliteration is presented in Yeshpanov et al. [2023]: a
system based on Tacotron 2 was trained only on Kazakh data, and to generate speech in other Turkic languages, letters
of the alphabets are mapped to International Phonetic Alphabet symbols, which are then converted into Kazakh letters.
Transfer learning from Kazakh for speech recognition of nine Turkic languages is studied in Orel et al. [2023]: most
models built using transfer learning outperformed monolingual baselines; the most significant improvement was achieved
for Yakut. Multilingual end-to-end speech recognition for five agglutinative Turkic languages with a common Cyrillic
script is presented in Bekarystankyzy et al. [2024]: multilingual training improved recognition scores for all languages
except Bashkir. In Bekarystankyzy et al. [2025], an integrated end-to-end multilingual speech recognition architecture
is proposed, using Connectionist Temporal Classification and an attention mechanism with a language model.
2.8
Sentiment Analysis and Text Classification
A software solution for building classifiers for natural language text analysis is presented in Smetanina et al. [2020]:
the task of sentiment analysis of Bashkir news texts is formulated, and a hybrid approach including dictionary-based
methods and machine-learning methods is proposed. In Suleimanov et al. [2020], a formal description of the term
“opinion”, the sentiment analysis task, approaches to its solution and ready-made software implementations are presented;
the developed solution based on the support vector machine with stochastic gradient descent demonstrated the highest
scores in terms of precision, recall and F-measure.
2.9
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
The methods of parameter-efficient fine-tuning arose as a response to the high cost of full fine-tuning of large language
models. LoRA Hu et al. [2022] freezes pretrained weights and adds trainable low-rank matrices, which reduces the
number of trainable parameters by several orders of magnitude. QLoRA Dettmers et al. [2023] extends this approach
with 4-bit quantisation to the NF4 format, which makes it possible to fine-tune models with billions of parameters on a
single GPU. DoRA Liu et al. [2024] decomposes pretrained weights into magnitude and direction, applying LoRA
only to the direction update; this decomposition increases the learning capacity and stability of the method. QDoRA
combines DoRA weight decomposition with 4-bit quantisation, which makes the method applicable under limited GPU
memory.
In this work, QLoRA is used among the methods listed above: it is implemented in all PEFT experiments and forms the
basis of the comparative analysis. Unquantised LoRA does not fit into 24 GB of NVIDIA L4 memory for 7–8B models
and was not run in the configuration reported here; DoRA and QDoRA are planned as an extension and require further
validation of compatibility with 4-bit quantisation in the Colab environment.
2.10
Application of PEFT to Turkic Languages
A comparative study of LoRA and QLoRA methods for the Bashkir language is presented in com [2026]: experiments
were conducted on a corpus of 71 thousand documents (46.9 million tokens) using DistilGPT2, GPT-2, Phi-2, Qwen2.5-
7B, DeepSeek-7B and Mistral-7B. The best perplexity was obtained for GPT-2 medium with full fine-tuning (3.34), while
QLoRA for Mistral-7B (3.79) and Phi-2 (3.81) achieved comparable quality with 40 times fewer trainable parameters.
Application of LoRA and QLoRA to the Tatar language is studied in Burnashev et al. [2026]: QLoRA adaptation of
Mistral-7B reached a perplexity of 3.98 on the test set, comparable to fully fine-tuned gpt2-medium (2.91). Mistral-7B
outperformed Qwen2.5-7B (perplexity 5.44) and DeepSeek-7B (perplexity 5.32) in adaptation to Tatar. Increasing the
LoRA rank from 8 to 16 consistently improved performance across all architectures.
Combining classical and transformer approaches for text classification and topic modelling of the Tatar language
is presented in Arabov et al. [2026a]: on a news corpus of 153,162 documents, classical approaches (TF-IDF+LR,
FastText+LR) and transformer architectures (mBERT, XLM-R, DistilBERT) were systematically compared on three
tasks — binary, multiclass (9 categories) and multilabel (13 categories).
Modelling subword embeddings for low-resource languages in the context of Industry 4.0 is presented in Arabov et al.
[2026c]: using Tatar as an example, a comparative analysis of Word2Vec and FastText architectures was carried out;
subword approaches using character n-grams significantly improved robustness to rare and unseen word forms.
A comprehensive study of Tatar text classification methods — from corpus creation and Tatar2Vec vector models to
benchmark construction — is presented in Arabov et al. [2026b]: the TatCorp 222M corpus, TatarTokenizers tokenisers,
Tatar2Vec models and an annotated dataset of 13,160 texts in ten topic categories were created. It was established that
the use of subword BPE tokenisation in the task of topic categorisation of an agglutinative language systematically
reduces classification quality.
