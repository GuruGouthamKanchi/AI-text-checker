Abstract
Recent advances in specialized decision mod-
els have expanded the possibilities for zero-
shot classification, yet their effectiveness on
Vietnamese remains underexplored.
This
study evaluates four specialized decision mod-
els across 13 classification settings spanning
six task groups, examining zero-shot perfor-
mance, sensitivity to label-description lan-
guage, and robustness to Vietnamese linguis-
tic variations. The evaluation considers En-
glish, Vietnamese, and bilingual label descrip-
tions, alongside common linguistic perturba-
tions involving teencode, typographical errors,
and diacritic removal. Under English label de-
scriptions, JEV achieves the strongest overall
performance, ranking first in 11 classification
settings. The effects of label-description lan-
guage vary across models and datasets, with
bilingual descriptions providing no consistent
improvement. Robustness experiments reveal
that diacritic removal causes the most substan-
tial performance degradation, reducing mean
macro-F1 by 9.51–15.47 points across model
configurations, considerably more than teen-
code or typographical errors. These findings
highlight the importance of label-description
choices and Vietnamese orthographic variation
when evaluating specialized decision models
for zero-shot text classification.

Introduction
Text classification is a fundamental task in natural
language processing, covering applications such
as sentiment analysis, intent detection, and nat-
ural language inference. General-purpose large
language models (LLMs) have demonstrated the
ability to perform such tasks through zero-shot
prompting. Recently, specialized decision mod-
els have emerged to provide structured predictions
over predefined candidate options without free-
form text generation. Models such as JEV supports
classification through typed decision interfaces or
user-defined schemas (TypeSafe AI, 2026).
Recent studies have evaluated specialized de-
cision models across various tasks and domains.
Deußer et al. (2026) evaluate JEV across 37
datasets, reporting strong performance on sev-
eral tasks alongside considerable variation in re-
sults. Other studies examine decision models and
compare their performance with general-purpose
LLMs (Duan et al., 2026; Ibrahim and Zaki, 2026).
Despite these efforts, their performance on Viet-
namese text classification remains insufficiently
characterized, particularly across tasks with differ-
ent label inventories and linguistic characteristics.
Classification performance may also depend on
how labels are specified and how input text is
written.
Previous research has shown that op-
tion labels can influence predictions in typed de-
cision models even when label definitions are pro-
vided (Azizi et al., 2026). This motivates exam-
ining the effects of label-description language on
Vietnamese classification tasks. Vietnamese user-
generated text also contains abbreviations, non-
standard spellings, and omitted diacritics (Nguyen
et al., 2024, 2023). Previous evaluations of Viet-
namese pretrained language models have reported
substantial performance degradation under diacritic
removal (Nguyen et al., 2023). These findings mo-
tivate further investigation of decision-model sen-
sitivity to both label descriptions and Vietnamese
linguistic variations.
This study evaluates four specialized decision
models across 13 Vietnamese classification set-
tings spanning six task groups, with additional
comparisons against previously published LLM
results. The evaluation examines zero-shot classifi-
cation performance, the effects of label-description
language, and robustness to Vietnamese linguistic
perturbations. Under English label descriptions,
JEV achieves the highest performance in 11 set-
tings. The effects of label-description language
vary across models and datasets, while diacritic re-
moval causes the largest performance degradation

Related Work
2.1
Specialized Decision Models and Their
Evaluation
Specialized decision models produce structured
predictions over predefined candidate options with-
out free-form text generation. JEV and LAYA pro-
vide typed decision interfaces, while GLiNER2.5-
multi-Decide supports multilingual classification
with user-specified labels (TypeSafe AI, 2026;
Convai Innovations, 2026; Zaratiana et al., 2025).
These systems support related classification tasks
despite differences in their architectures and infer-
ence interfaces.
Recent evaluations show that decision-model
performance varies across tasks and evaluation
conditions. Broad benchmarks report competi-
tive results on several classification and knowledge
tasks, alongside limitations on more demanding
tasks and settings (Deußer et al., 2026; Duan et al.,
2026; Ibrahim and Zaki, 2026; Li et al., 2026).
More focused studies reveal behavior not captured
by aggregate scores. Experiments on legal infer-
ence identify sensitivity to request configuration,
comparisons of JEV and LAYA show different per-
formance and robustness profiles, and analyses of
JEV report biases in the distribution of selected
labels and ordinal scores (Zhang et al., 2026; Li,
2026; Gao et al., 2026). However, evidence across
diverse Vietnamese classification tasks remains
limited.
2.2
Label Specifications and Vietnamese
Linguistic Robustness
The specification of candidate labels can influ-
ence zero-shot classification. Studies of generative
LLMs show that label wording and definitions af-
fect classification performance (Mu et al., 2024; Li
et al., 2025). Related findings have been reported
for typed decision models, where option names can
exert greater influence than their accompanying
definitions (Azizi et al., 2026). This evidence mo-
tivates examining the language of label descriptions
as a separate factor in Vietnamese classification.
Robustness evaluation examines model behav-
ior under controlled changes to input text. Check-
List and TextFlint establish methods for testing
linguistic transformations, while DecisionRobust
extends perturbation-based evaluation to decision
models and emphasizes the importance of vali-
dating that transformed inputs retain their original
labels (Ribeiro et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2021; Lan
et al., 2026). For Vietnamese, ViLexNorm docu-
ments nonstandard lexical forms in social media,
and ViSoBERT reports performance degradation
under diacritic removal (Nguyen et al., 2024, 2023).
These findings motivate evaluating the effects of
Vietnamese linguistic variations on specialized de-
cision models.

3.3
Zero-Shot Evaluation Protocol
All models are evaluated with frozen weights, with-
out task-specific fine-tuning or labeled demonstra-
tions. Each model receives the task input and its
predefined candidate labels. To examine the effects
of label-description language, three configurations
are considered: English (EN), Vietnamese (VI),
and bilingual English–Vietnamese (EN+VI). La-
bel identities and their order remain fixed across
configurations, while EN+VI combines the English
and Vietnamese descriptions. English descriptions
serve as the reference configuration for the main
evaluation.
Macro-F1 is the primary evaluation metric, with
accuracy and weighted-F1 reported as supplemen-
tary measures. Macro-F1 is computed over classes

4.4
Effects of Label-Description Language
Table 2 shows distinct patterns of sensitivity to
label-description language across models. JEV ex-
hibits the most stable average performance, with
macro-F1 varying by only 0.35 points across the
three configurations and reaching its highest value
under EN+VI descriptions (69.44).
Quyet and
LAYA achieve their highest average scores with
Vietnamese descriptions, although the improve-
ments over English are modest and both models
perform substantially worse with bilingual descrip-
tions. GLiNER, in contrast, performs best with En-
glish descriptions and exhibits the largest average
degradation under VI and EN+VI. These results
suggest that multilingual label descriptions do not
necessarily benefit models built on multilingual
encoders.
The effects are also strongly task-dependent.
Quyet, for example, gains 21.06 macro-F1 points
on ViNLI with Vietnamese descriptions, largely
through improved discrimination between other
and neutral. However, the same configuration re-
duces its performance on ViHSD by 14.23 points,
accompanied by increased misclassification of
clean examples as offensive. Additional instance-
level prediction comparisons are provided in Ap-
pendix A.1. This contrast suggests that the effect
of description language is closely related to the dis-
tinctions between task-specific labels, rather than
a general preference for Vietnamese descriptions.
The degradation observed for LAYA and GLiNER
is particularly pronounced in some settings. LAYA
loses 18.70 macro-F1 points on ViMQ with bilin-
gual descriptions, while GLiNER assigns other to
every ViNLI example under both Vietnamese and
bilingual descriptions. These findings indicate that

Robustness to Vietnamese Linguistic
Variation
5.1
Perturbation Design
We consider three types of Vietnamese linguis-
tic variation. Teencode introduces informal lexical
substitutions commonly found in online commu-
nication, such as “không” →“ko” (“not”). Ty-
pographical noise simulates typing errors through
Telex-related substitutions, diacritic modifications,
and character-level edits. Diacritic removal re-
moves Vietnamese tone and vowel diacritics, in-
cluding the conversion of đ to d. Each perturbation
is applied independently to the original test inputs
while retaining the original labels. For NLI, teen-
code and diacritic removal affect both the premise
and hypothesis, whereas typographical noise is ap-
plied only to the hypothesis.

character edit burden across the 13 evaluation set-
